# The live tutor draws, research

The operator asked on 2026-09-30 for the live tutor to draw whatever figure best teaches the moment, built the way a teacher builds a sketch on a whiteboard while explaining, step by step in time with the words. A second instruction the same day widened the vocabulary beyond graphs: shapes such as circles, the different kinds of triangle, arrows and boxes, free text labels, and different kinds of stroke.

Four strands were researched in parallel by Opus 5.5 agents and synthesised here by the orchestrator: teaching practice and products, learning science, safe drawing with stream synchronisation and accessibility, and an inventory of the repository. Two claims were checked again by the orchestrator against the source text (the SVGO advisory and the Fiorella 2019 effect sizes), and one source was added (Brysbaert 2019 on reading rate). Every web claim carries its URL and the access date 2026-09-30. A claim is [verified] only where two independent sources agree, and papers from one research group count as one source. One source is [single-source], the writer's reasoning is [inferred], unknowns are [uncertain]. A heading carries the weakest tag its section relies on. Repository facts were read from branch `agent/drawing` and cite the file. No source is quoted beyond 25 words.

## How skilled teachers build a figure at the board [single-source]

The board is planned and kept. Japanese board work (bansho) is planned before the lesson by solving the problem and anticipating student solutions, is read left to right from the problem to the summary, and stays whole to the end of the lesson as the record students consult (https://lessonresearch.net/teaching-problem-solving/boardwork/, 2026-09-30). An observer of Japanese classrooms reports that teachers added elements in a strategic sequence and rarely erased except at a major instructional transition (https://larrycuban.wordpress.com/2015/04/26/lessons-learned-from-a-chalkboard-slow-and-steady-technology-integration-bradley-emerling/, 2026-09-30). That the board persists and grows in sequence is [verified] across the two. A study of a Japanese mathematics lesson found that an abrupt leap in abstraction between adjacent representations on the board hindered pupils (https://zenodo.org/records/5758589, 2026-09-30) [single-source], which argues against skipping the intermediate step in a figure built in steps [inferred].

Teachers point to link words to the figure. In six middle-school mathematics lessons, teachers produced 13.08 gestures per 100 words in the turn after a student showed confusion against 7.37 before it, and 62 to 64 percent of the gestures were points (https://website.education.wisc.edu/mnathan/Publications_files/2013%20Alibali_etal%20ZDM%20Teacher%20Gesture%20Trouble%20Spots.pdf, 2026-09-30). Teachers in the higher-scoring regions of the TIMSS video study kept the source representation visible during a comparison, used aligned layout and arrows, and pointed back and forth, with spatial supports more than twice as often as in US lessons (http://learninglab.uchicago.edu/Publications_files/6%20Richland%20(2007)%20Cog%20supports%20for%20Analogies.pdf, 2026-09-30). That teachers use pointing and arrows to link representations is [verified] across the two. More is not always better: 82 students learned less when a teacher gestured at the equations than when she did not, while gesturing at the graphs carried no cost (https://d-nb.info/1144373824/34, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. In GeoDial, 1,341 geometry tutoring dialogues by experienced educators, about 45 percent of teacher utterances carried a highlight on the diagram, and fine-tuned models placed highlights badly (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12419, 2026-09-30) [single-source].

Colour carries a fixed meaning where it is used. One bansho guide gives white for standard writing, yellow for key points, and blue and red for contrasting sides (https://www.teachermagazine.com/au_en/articles/enhancing-your-teaching-with-traditional-bansho-board-writing, 2026-09-30), and the Japanese classroom observer saw consistent chalk colour meanings, though outside mathematics (Emerling, URL above). Consistent colour roles are [verified] as a practice. How many colours is contested: one lecturer's guide allows two at most and asks for complete erasure so residue is not read as new material (https://peternewbury.org/2015/01/14/chalkboard-tips/, 2026-09-30), while a calculus teacher uses five to ten to separate f, f prime and f double prime (https://www.stuartschool.org/admissions/blog/posts/~board/blog-posts/post/how-color-and-creativity-transform-the-way-students-connect-ideas-in-calculus, 2026-09-30) [single-source each].

Annotation marks are few and familiar: arrows connecting related ideas and blank space left for what nobody anticipated (Tan, URL above), and in animated mathematics the three families Manim names, creation (draw, write, show parts in turn and keep them), transformation (turn one object into another, with or without keeping the original) and indication (circumscribe, flash, focus) (https://docs.manim.community/en/stable/reference/manim.animation.indication.html, 2026-09-30) [single-source].

## How strong products build a figure while explaining [single-source]

Khan Academy videos are freehand drawing on an electronic blackboard recorded with narration (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Academy and https://www.teachthought.com/technology/how-to-screencast/, 2026-09-30) [verified]. Across 6.9 million edX sessions, students engaged 1.5 to 2 times as long with tablet-drawn tutorials in that style as with slides or code screencasts, which measures engagement and not learning (https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf, 2026-09-30) [single-source].

GeoGebra records every construction as an ordered list, the construction protocol, with a navigation bar that steps through it (first, back, forward, last), shows the current step of the total, groups several objects into one step with breakpoints, and can play the construction (https://geogebra.github.io/docs/manual/en/Construction_Protocol/ and https://geogebra.github.io/docs/manual/en/Navigation_Bar/, 2026-09-30) [single-source, one vendor]. It is the closest existing model of a figure built in named steps with learner control. The Desmos API builds a graph one expression at a time with an id, colour, line style (solid, dashed, dotted), label and a hidden flag, so step reveal is a hidden toggle; its saved state is JSON the documentation says to treat as opaque, and it needs an API key (https://www.desmos.com/api/v1.11/docs/index.html, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. Photomath Plus offers more than 400 animated tutorials that simulate a teacher at a whiteboard with voice-over (https://support.google.com/photomath/answer/14330572?hl=en-GB, 2026-09-30) [single-source].

Model-drawn figures shipped in 2025 and 2026. Claude's custom visuals, launched 12 March 2026, are HTML built inside the chat (https://claude.com/blog/claude-builds-visuals and https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13979539-custom-visuals-in-chat-and-cowork, 2026-09-30) [verified], and artifacts run in a sandboxed iframe on a separate `claudeusercontent.com` origin under a strict content security policy (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/artifacts and https://pluto.security/blog/inside-claude-artifacts-how-your-agent-publishes-to-the-web/, 2026-09-30) [verified]; the same independent test found `unsafe-inline` and `unsafe-eval` in that frame, so the isolation is the origin and not a limit on the code [single-source]. Google's generative UI has Gemini write HTML, CSS and JavaScript per prompt, sometimes taking over a minute (https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. Khanmigo has used Gemini since 27 August 2026 to generate an interactive diagram when a visual would help, with preliminary gains in engagement and next-item correctness reported by Khan Academy (https://blog.khanacademy.org/new-ai-tools-bring-interactive-diagrams-and-targeted-practice-thanks-to-khan-academys-partnership-with-google-org/ and https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/khan-academy-brings-gemini-powered-visual-tutoring-into-classrooms-for-2026, 2026-09-30) [verified]; how those diagrams are specified is not stated [uncertain]. ChatGPT added interactive visual explanations for more than 70 mathematics and science topics in March 2026 (https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/chatgpt-can-now-create-interactive-visuals-to-help-you-understand-math-and-science-concepts and https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/openai-introduces-interactive-learning-tools-for-stem-topics-in-chatgpt, 2026-09-30) [verified]; whether they are prebuilt or generated per request is disputed between the two [uncertain].

Research systems show where model drawing fails. An agent writing Manim code rendered 93.8 percent of 240 theorem videos, most with minor layout problems (https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.332/, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. A TikZ pipeline found overlapping elements, wrong labels or measurements and uneven spacing as the recurring errors (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09839, 2026-09-30) [single-source].

## Dynamic and static figures in the learning science [single-source]

Three independent meta-analyses agree that animation beats static graphics by a small amount on average, between about 0.23 and 0.37 [verified]: Hoffler and Leutner 2007, 26 studies and 76 comparisons, d = 0.37 (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ780451, 2026-09-30); Berney and Betrancourt 2016, 61 studies, k = 140, N = 7,036, g = 0.226 (https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/75bf6876-cb91-4e2c-8ac6-9d0767449449/download, 2026-09-30); Castro-Alonso and colleagues 2019, 46 studies, N = 5,474, g = 0.23 (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1217424, 2026-09-30).

The moderators in Berney and Betrancourt bear directly on a tutor panel, and each is [single-source]. Abstract representations, which that paper defines to include graphs, diagrams and formal notation, gave g = 0.006 (k = 26). Mathematics gave g = -0.067 (k = 11). Written commentary gave g = 0.105, not significant (k = 58), against spoken commentary g = 0.320 (k = 28). The advantage appeared only with system pacing, g = 0.309 (k = 96); full learner control (rewind, fast-forward, repeat) sat at g = -0.120 (k = 12). Hoffler and Leutner found representational animation at d = 0.40 and procedural-motor knowledge at d = 1.06 (ERIC record above).

Motion itself is not the benefit. Tversky, Morrison and Betrancourt found that where animation beat static graphics the animated version usually carried more information or added interactivity, that animations are often too fast or complex to perceive, and that people conceive continuous change as discrete steps (https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs448b/papers/Tversky_AnimationFacilitate_IJHCS02.pdf, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. Kalyuga found novices learned graphs of linear and quadratic transformations better from static diagrams and more knowledgeable learners from animated ones (https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.chb.2007.02.018, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. Transience costs working memory when sections are long (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ978021, 2026-09-30) [single-source].

Calculus-specific controlled evidence is thin. No controlled experiment compared an animated with a static figure for secant to tangent, Riemann sums, slope fields, limits, parametric or polar curves, or series [uncertain]. A grade 8 and 9 study of the function concept found dynamic figures beat static ones (adjusted means 7.20 and 7.34 against 6.12, p = .02) with no difference between watched and interactive dynamic figures (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7212367/, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. GeoGebra meta-analyses report g = 0.653 over 14 studies with larger effects for calculus (https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2023.2250886, 2026-09-30) and g = 0.96 over 29 studies with an asymmetric funnel plot and a likely novelty effect (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8113830/, 2026-09-30); both compare with conventional teaching, not with the same figure shown static [single-source each].

## Watching a figure being drawn [single-source]

The direct evidence comes from one group, so it counts once. In Fiorella, Stull, Kuhlmann and Mayer 2019, Experiment 1, 66 undergraduates watched a 12-minute kidney lecture; drawings created while the instructor spoke, with neither instructor nor hand visible, beat the same drawings already made, d = .54 (95 percent CI .05 to 1.03), with the gain on retention, d = .66, and transfer not significant, d = .31 (https://psycnet.apa.org/manuscript/2018-58542-001.pdf, 2026-09-30, read by the orchestrator from the manuscript text). In Fiorella and Mayer 2016, a 100-second Doppler lesson, a visible hand gave d = 0.35 (N = 121) and drawings appearing with no hand gave d = -0.16 (N = 107) (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1099178, 2026-09-30). Mayer, Fiorella and Stull 2020 state the dynamic drawing principle, that people learn better when the instructor draws while speaking than from an already-drawn graphic (https://ugc.futurelearn.com/uploads/files/dd/53/dd539a50-07ba-4e34-9794-29f357706b86/Five_ways_to_increase_the_effectiveness_of_instructional_video_Mayer_et_al_2020.pdf, 2026-09-30), and explain it by signalling, segmenting and temporal contiguity. An independent group found progressive drawing beat static pictures on retention with 133 secondary students (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9898452/fullTextXML, 2026-09-30), so the direction of the effect is [verified] across two groups. The visible hand is unsettled: it hurt in one physics study (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1136843, 2026-09-30) and was neutral for learning in two later studies (https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70156 and https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1186/s40561-023-00258-6, 2026-09-30) [single-source each]. Computer diagrams animated to match the act of drawing gave d = 0.33, not significant (the 2015 dissertation, https://escholarship.org/content/qt55v7s9t1/qt55v7s9t1_noSplash_87418a79f71257344483b22e7c67c540.pdf?t=prk1a3, 2026-09-30) [single-source].

What the student does after a drawing matters more than whether they draw. With 196 students, writing an explanation after a segment beat drawing (transfer d = 0.67) and rewatching (transfer d = 0.87), and explaining was strongest after dynamic drawings (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1259094, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. That matches the tutor's existing self-explanation move.

Every one of these studies used spoken narration. Growth's tutor writes; no study was found in which a figure is drawn in step with written text [uncertain].

## Contiguity, segmenting, pacing and signalling [single-source]

Integration of words and figure is [verified] across two meta-analyses: Ginns 2006, 50 studies, with complexity moderating both contiguity effects (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ747512, 2026-09-30), and Schroeder and Cenkci 2018, 58 comparisons, n = 2,426, g = 0.63 (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1186641, 2026-09-30), which plan 08 already cites. Short on-figure labels of two or three words raised retention (d = 0.47 and 0.70) and not transfer (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ796353, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. The boundary that matters here: temporal contiguity vanished or reversed when words were printed rather than spoken, because printed words and a moving picture compete for the eyes (https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. The figure should therefore be still while a sentence is being read [inferred].

Segmenting is [verified] as an ERIC record and the full text agree on its scope: Rey and colleagues 2019, 56 investigations, 88 comparisons (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1217373 and https://maria-wirzberger.de/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Rey2019_Article_AMeta-analysisOfTheSegmentingE.pdf, 2026-09-30). From the full text [single-source]: retention d = 0.32 (k = 67) and transfer d = 0.36 (k = 56), a repeat option lifting transfer to d = 0.55 (k = 12). The classic segmented lesson ran one or two sentences per segment. Learner control across educational technology averaged g = 0.05 over 18 studies (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1034262, 2026-09-30) [single-source].

Signalling helped in 24 of 28 tests, median d = 0.41; spreading colour cues gave d = 0.75 while arrow cues gave d = -0.03, and cues tracing a progressive path gave d = 1.42 (Mayer and Fiorella chapter, URL above) [single-source]. Colour coding sped the location of matching information between text and illustration (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ842989, 2026-09-30), and learning with colour then being tested without it gave the worst results (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1336817 and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8576459/, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. Integration signals helped low prior knowledge 8th graders and hurt high prior knowledge ones (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1178409, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. Assistance helps novices and costs experts, d = 0.505 and -0.428 over 60 studies (https://www.pedocs.de/frontdoor.php?la=en&source_opus=34113&nr=1&anker=start&zeitschriftentitel=Learning+and+instruction&zeitschriftenjahr=2025&zeitschriftenband=98, 2026-09-30) [single-source].

Reading pace sets how long a sentence stays in front of the student before the next step. Adults read English non-fiction silently at 238 words per minute on average, most between 175 and 300, over 190 studies and 18,573 participants, and school-age readers read more slowly (https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/linguistics/2019-brysbaert.pdf, 2026-09-30, read by the orchestrator from the paper text) [single-source]. Streamed at the 85 to 115 output tokens per second measured for the tutor's model (https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-sonnet-5-5-low, 2026-09-30) [single-source], a sentence arrives in well under a second, so a figure revealed at generation speed would finish before the student has read its first sentence [inferred].

## Why a figure before submission is a hint [single-source]

In the Geometry Cognitive Tutor, whose last hint conveys the answer, control students reached it on 70 percent of hint sequences (https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/Web/People/bmclaren/pubs/RollEtAl-ImprovingHelpSeekingWithITS-LandI2011.pdf, 2026-09-30) [single-source], and better help seeking did not raise domain learning (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1091255, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. Problem solving before instruction beat instruction first, g = 0.36 over 53 studies (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1308129, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. Item-writing guidance treats a clue to the key as a flaw (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ660246, 2026-09-30) [single-source], and College Board's CLEP directions say figures are drawn as accurately as possible unless stated (https://clep.collegeboard.org/clep-exams/calculus, 2026-09-30) [single-source], so students are trained to read values from an accurate figure. LLM tutor leakage research measured up to 74 percent mean leakage under contextual manipulation (https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18660v1, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. No study of leakage through a tutor's own figure was found [uncertain]. Before an item is checked, then, a figure may show what the stem gives and a neutral scaffold, and never the element that carries the answer [inferred].

## Whiteboard shapes, text labels and strokes [inferred]

A graph vocabulary alone cannot draw the related-rates triangle, the cone of a draining tank, a box-and-arrow picture of rate in and rate out, a brace naming a width, or a circle around the point a sentence is about, and the operator asked for exactly those on 2026-09-30. The teaching strand's annotation set (arrows linking ideas, a ring around a point or region, a bracket or brace for a length, a highlight on the element being discussed) and the inventory's geometric needs (right-angle marker, angle arc, circle, labelled lengths, a varying length) give the list: circle and arc, rectangle or box with an optional text inside, triangle by kind (right, isosceles, equilateral, scalene), polygon, arrow with a head at either or both ends, brace, angle and right-angle marks, congruence ticks, a callout label with a leader line to the thing it names, and free text labels that may hold inline mathematics.

Two properties from the research decide how these are offered. Models place coordinates badly (next section), so a triangle is asked for by kind and size (a right triangle with legs 3 and 4 at a corner, an isosceles triangle with base 4 and height 3), and the server computes the vertices, the right-angle mark and the equal-side ticks. And meaning belongs to a role, not to a colour the model picks, because Growth's accent is monochrome and colour must never carry meaning alone (see "What Growth already has").

Strokes: a board has chalk of different weights, dashed construction lines and a highlighter. The allowed strokes are solid, dashed and dotted, in three weights that map to the three stroke tokens (`stroke-hairline` 1, `stroke-mark` 1.5, `stroke-curve` 2 in `app/design/tokens.py`), arrowheads at the end, the start or both, and a highlighter, a wide translucent underlay in an accent tint drawn behind an element to mark it as the one being discussed. Decorative strokes (hand-drawn wobble, textures, shadows) are left out, because plan 08's coherence rule and its no-gradient rule both apply and coherence held in 23 of 23 tests (Mayer and Fiorella chapter, URL above).

## Declarative specs against model-written markup or code [verified]

Every product that lets a model write HTML, SVG or JavaScript runs it on a separate origin under a restrictive policy (the Claude artifacts sources above; OpenAI's Apps SDK runs widgets in an isolated iframe with a strict policy, https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/guides/security-privacy, 2026-09-30). Growth serves one origin with `script-src 'self'` and no `unsafe-eval` (`app/api/security_headers.py`), and plan 09 already rules that a figure is a declarative specification drawn from a fixed vocabulary and never SVG the model wrote (`docs/plan/09-security-and-privacy.md`, Rendering).

SVG is a document format with several execution paths that sanitisers keep missing. Script inside `foreignObject` is an XSS path in two independent cases, SVGO's `removeScripts` (CVE-2026-84369, https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-4vpr-x523-8j87, 2026-09-30, confirmed by the orchestrator) and Firefox (CVE-2022-28284, https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1754522, 2026-09-30) [verified]. `animate` and `set` can change an attribute to a script URL after a parse-time sanitiser has run (https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-5hc8-qmg8-pw27, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. Attribute selectors with `url()` requests leak page data without script (https://portswigger.net/research/blind-css-exfiltration and https://owasp.org/www-project-web-security-testing-guide/v41/4-Web_Application_Security_Testing/11-Client_Side_Testing/05-Testing_for_CSS_Injection, 2026-09-30) [verified], and Growth's policy allows inline style attributes for MathLive, so a model-written `style` would reach that path. XML parsing adds billion laughs, quadratic blowup and external entities (https://github.com/tiran/defusedxml/blob/main/README.md and https://docs.python.org/3/library/xml.html, 2026-09-30) [verified].

Third-party drawing languages carry their own record. Vega lists ten advisories from December 2020 to January 2026, most of them XSS through its expression language (https://github.com/vega/vega/security, 2026-09-30), including CVE-2023-26486 (https://github.com/vega/vega/security/advisories/GHSA-4vq7-882g-wcg4 and https://security.snyk.io/vuln/SNYK-JS-VEGA-3338522, 2026-09-30) [verified], and its default expression parser needs `unsafe-eval` (https://vega.github.io/vega/usage/interpreter/, 2026-09-30). Mermaid's ten most recent advisories span August 2025 to August 2026 (https://github.com/mermaid-js/mermaid/security, 2026-09-30). Plotly.js before 2.25.2 had a critical prototype pollution flaw (https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-wjc4-73q6-gv3m and https://security.snyk.io/vuln/SNYK-JS-PLOTLYJS-6142157, 2026-09-30) [verified]. GeoGebra files can carry JavaScript that runs on load (https://geogebra.github.io/docs/manual/en/Scripting/, 2026-09-30), and Desmos state is opaque (URL above). A closed schema rendered by the application's own code has a smaller surface than any of them [inferred].

## How accurately models produce figures [single-source]

Models choose what to draw well and produce exact geometry and numbers badly. GPT-4 wrote valid plotting code 96.71 percent of the time but only 75.27 percent passed validity and legality together over 2,524 queries (https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00981, 2026-09-30). SVG accuracy fell from 80.25 to 33.33 percent from easy to hard items for Claude 3.7 Sonnet (https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03139v1, 2026-09-30). Olympiad geometry diagrams in Asymptote compiled 36.14 percent of the time, and the authors conclude that solving does not imply drawing (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18111, 2026-09-30). Moving arithmetic to an interpreter helps: PAL gained 15 points on GSM8K and Program of Thoughts about 12 percent over chain of thought (https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10435 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12588, 2026-09-30) [verified]. So the model writes expressions, references ("the point on curve f at x = 2") and sizes, and code computes every sampled point, every y value on a curve, every tangent slope, every region boundary and every triangle vertex [inferred]. Computed points are exact, cost fewer tokens than coordinate lists, and are the only form a server can leak-check reliably.

## Validation, caps and expression safety [verified]

Constrained decoding cannot enforce the caps this needs: Anthropic structured outputs reject `minimum`, `maximum`, `maxLength` and array limits beyond `minItems` 0 or 1 (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs, 2026-09-30), and the CLI's `--json-schema` returns JSON only after the reply completes (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cli-reference, 2026-09-30), so it cannot interleave a figure with streamed sentences [single-source each]. The tutor has no tools, which rules out streamed tool arguments. Server-side validation is the enforcement point [inferred]. Python `jsonschema` supports draft 2020-12 (https://python-jsonschema.readthedocs.io/en/stable/validate/, 2026-09-30) and Growth already validates the screen shape with `Draft202012Validator` (`app/agent/context.py`). CPython's JSON decoder raises `RecursionError`, not `ValueError`, on deep nesting (https://github.com/jpadilla/pyjwt/security/advisories/GHSA-8wjv-2p76-3863, 2026-09-30) [single-source], so a character cap and a caught `RecursionError` are both needed.

Python's `eval` executes arbitrary code (https://docs.python.org/3/library/functions.html), and SymPy's `sympify` and `parse_expr` use `eval` while `lambdify` uses `exec`, each documented as unsafe on unsanitised input (https://docs.sympy.org/latest/modules/parsing.html and https://docs.sympy.org/latest/modules/utilities/lambdify.html, 2026-09-30) [verified]. `app/generation/kit.py` samples with `sympify` and `lambdify`, which is acceptable for trusted templates and never for tutor text. The client already has a closed grammar with no `eval`, `app/web/src/lessons/expression.ts`: numbers, names from a table of 19 functions, `+ - * / ^ **`, parentheses, and constants pi, e and oo, with no cap on input length or depth. math.js, which no longer uses `eval`, still advises killing untrusted evaluation on time or memory (https://mathjs.org/docs/expressions/security.html, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. Once code execution is gone, the remaining risks are rendering-cost bombs (a huge sample count, a window 10^9 wide, a fine slope-field lattice, deep nesting, thousands of labels), each closed by a fixed cap [inferred].

## What leaks an answer in a figure [inferred]

The channels, each checkable by code once the server has computed the points:

1. A label, a step caption or the description that states the key, the failure the sentence screen already catches in prose.
2. A marked point, a segment end, a label anchor, a number-line point or interval end, or a sign-chart critical value whose coordinate equals a numeric key form.
3. A line, secant or tangent whose slope or intercept equals the key, for derivative and linearisation items.
4. A shaded region, a Riemann sum or a trapezoid sum whose total equals the key, for integral and approximation items.
5. A drawn curve equal to a key expression, such as the graph of f prime when the item asks for f prime, or a constant curve at the key's value.
6. On a multiple-choice item, a label naming the key letter or the key option's value.
7. A table cell equal to the key.

An eighth channel, a gridline or tick falling exactly on the key while a curve's feature sits on it, is the fuzziest and is left to the evals before it gates [uncertain]. Integers need the same treatment the text screen gives a math span: compared, not ignored, because a marked point at x = 2 is how a figure would leak "the maximum is at x = 2". The cost is a false positive when a generic sketch happens to use the key's integer, and a false positive withholds a reply that did not leak, which errs in the safe direction [inferred].

## Keeping a drawing in step with streamed text [inferred]

Products carry structure beside text either as typed events or as inline markup. The Vercel AI SDK stream has text parts, tool parts with streamed input, and custom `data-*` parts (https://ai-sdk.dev/docs/ai-sdk-ui/stream-protocol, 2026-09-30); Anthropic streams tool arguments as unvalidated `input_json_delta` fragments to be parsed at the block's end (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/fine-grained-tool-streaming, 2026-09-30); LibreChat embeds an artifact directive in Markdown (https://mintlify.wiki/danny-avila/librechat/features/artifacts, 2026-09-30); Streamdown waits for an unclosed fence to close before an expensive render (https://github.com/vercel/streamdown/blob/main/packages/streamdown/CHANGELOG.md, 2026-09-30); Chrome's guidance is to stop rendering when unsafe output is detected rather than show a sanitised version (https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/render-llm-responses, 2026-09-30) [single-source each]. Growth's CLI path yields only text deltas and the tutor has no tools, so inline markup parsed on the server, then re-emitted as typed events, is the only route.

Three placements were compared. A whole figure first costs its generation time once (a compact six-step block of about 800 characters is about 200 to 270 tokens, 2 to 3 seconds at the measured speed) and lets the whole figure be screened before any part shows, but ties nothing to the words. Step blocks between sentences give the finest timing, but the first step shows before the later ones exist, so a leak that appears only in combination cannot be caught and each fence is another chance for a malformed block. One block holding every step, followed by short step markers in the prose, keeps the whole-figure check, pays the latency once and synchronises at sentence granularity, which is the finest the sentence screen permits, since it releases nothing smaller than a sentence.

The splitter must sit before `SentenceScreen.feed` in `screened_text` (`app/agent/turn.py`). If block text reached the screen, a blank line inside the JSON would end a sentence, decimals such as 0.5 would feed the plain-decimal comparison in `app/evals/agent_checks.py`, and a key value inside the JSON would withhold the whole reply by mistake. At an opening fence the text already buffered must be screened and released first, so text and figure keep their order. The client's 15-second first-text timer (`useAgentStream.ts`) must also be cleared by a figure event, since a block before the first sentence delays the first text.

Generation speed and reading speed differ by an order of magnitude (previous sections), so "in time with the words" has two readings: in time with the words' arrival, which finishes the figure before the first sentence is read, or in time with the words' reading. Only the second matches a teacher who draws as they speak [inferred].

## Accessibility of a figure that appears over time [single-source]

The WCAG 2.2 criteria that bind (https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/, 2026-09-30): 1.1.1 asks a chart for a short alternative and a long description of its data (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-content.html); 1.4.11 asks 3:1 for graphics needed for understanding against the adjacent colour, and Chartability rates geometry under 3:1 a critical failure (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html and https://github.com/Chartability/POUR-CAF) [verified]; 2.2.2 asks for pause, stop or hide on movement that starts on its own and lasts over five seconds (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html); 2.3.3 lets motion from interaction be disabled unless essential, with technique C39 and `prefers-reduced-motion` (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39 and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion) [verified]; 3.2.5 counts a change of focus as a change of context, so a figure appearing must not take focus from the composer (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/change-on-request.html); 4.1.3 covers status messages (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html).

For inline SVG, `role="img"` with `aria-labelledby` pointing at a title gave the most consistent screen reader support, and problems concentrated in SVGs inside links and buttons (https://vispero.com/resources/using-aria-enhance-svg-accessibility/ and https://www.scottohara.me/blog/2019/05/22/contextual-images-svgs-and-a11y.html, 2026-09-30) [verified]. WAI-ARIA 1.2 gives `img` presentational children, and live regions wait while `aria-busy` is set (https://www.w3.org/TR/wai-aria-1.2/ and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Guides/Live_regions, 2026-09-30) [verified], so per-step text inside the SVG is unreachable and belongs in a list outside it, and steps and sentences are not announced piece by piece while the reply is busy. The NCAM guidance for STEM images asks for a title, a brief summary then detail, and tables rather than narrative for data (https://www.wgbh.org/foundation/services/ncam/tools-resources/effective-practices-for-description-of-science-content-guidelines-for-describing-stem-images, 2026-09-30) [single-source]. A figure built in steps is a narrative, and its ordered step list is its accessible form [inferred].

## Where the evidence and Growth's rules pull apart [inferred]

- Narration against writing. Every dynamic drawing result used speech, and written commentary with animation gave a non-significant g = 0.105. Growth's tutor writes. The response is to keep the figure still while a sentence is read, reveal a step only with its sentence, and pace reveals to reading rather than to generation.
- Pacing. System pacing carried the animation advantage (g = 0.309) and full learner control did not (g = -0.120), while a repeat option helped segmenting (d = 0.55). The build is therefore paced by the system, reading speed, and the student gets step controls afterwards, not instead.
- Plan 08's 300 ms rule against a build lasting several seconds. The rule governs a transition. A figure step appearing with its sentence is content arriving, like streamed text, and each step's own transition stays at 300 ms ease-out on transform and opacity. No duration longer than 300 ms is needed and none is added.
- Plan 08's ban on a staggered list reveal. That ban targets decorative staggering on load. A step here waits for the sentence that explains it, which is the temporal contiguity the plan's own figure rule rests on. The amendment says so explicitly.
- The brief's provisional reduced-motion path, the finished figure with a step list. Reduced motion asks for movement to be replaced, and plan 08 says replace, not delete. The benefit rides on segmenting and contiguity, not on motion, so under reduced motion the build stays in step with the words, every transform is dropped, the step list is shown open, and the finished figure with its list is the end state, as the brief asked.
- Colour for meaning against a monochrome accent. The operator's tokens give one ink for curves and marks, and plan 08 allows exactly two semantic colours, which differ only by hue. The four roles therefore ride on ink lightness, weight, dash and the highlighter tint, with `state-incorrect` kept for the error role only and always with a word. Skulmowski's finding that learning with colour and testing without it does worst points the same way.
- Draw-on strokes. Plan 08 allows only transform and opacity, so `stroke-dashoffset` tracing is out. A 300 ms wipe of a step's strokes by a transformed clip keeps within the rule and is removed under reduced motion; the evidence gives it no learning value of its own (d = 0.33, not significant), so it is there for the operator's whiteboard brief and nothing depends on it.

## Ranked synthesis [inferred]

1. **The drawing language is a closed, declarative figure spec the model writes as one fenced `figure` block of JSON inside its streamed reply.** Kinds: `graph` (axes, a window), `diagram` (no axes, an equal-scale canvas), `number_line` and `table`. Elements: function curve, parametric and polar curve, point (free or on a named curve at an x), line (through two points or point and slope), segment, secant (curve and two x values), tangent (curve and x), vertical and horizontal guide, area under or between curves on an interval, Riemann rectangles (left, right, midpoint) and trapezoids, slope field with an optional solution through a point, Euler steps, sequence and partial-sum dots, vector, circle, arc and angle mark, rectangle or box with optional inner text, triangle by kind (right, isosceles, equilateral, scalene), polygon, brace, text label, callout with a leader line, sign-chart row, interval, and table row, cell and column highlights. Every element has an id, a role (given, constructed, highlight, error), an optional label and an optional stroke (solid, dashed or dotted; thin, regular or bold; arrowheads; highlighter). The model writes expressions, references and sizes; it never writes a coordinate list, a colour, markup, a style or code. Rejected: model-written SVG, HTML, CSS or JavaScript (plan 09 and the SVG record), a third-party language such as Vega-Lite, Mermaid, Plotly, Desmos state or GeoGebra commands (security records, expression languages, opaque state, embedded scripts), point lists written by the model (accuracy), and tool calls (the no-tools rule and the CLI path).
2. **The rendering path is compile, screen, render.** The server parses the block (`json.loads` under a character cap, `RecursionError` caught), validates it against `schemas/agent/figure.schema.json` and semantic caps, parses every expression with a Python twin of `expression.ts` (capped length, node count and depth, no names outside the table), computes every point, and produces a render spec in an extended `GraphFigureSpec` shape where every element carries its step, role and stroke. The screen checks that render spec and its texts. The client receives only the render spec, re-validates it and draws it with an extended `FigureView`, with text labels in an HTML layer typeset by the tutor's KaTeX renderer. No model string becomes a tag, attribute name, URL or style. Rejected: client-side evaluation of model expressions, because then what the screen checked and what the student sees could differ.
3. **The animation model is a stepped build synchronised with sentences and paced to reading.** The figure is a sequence of named steps. The prose carries a marker, `[[step:name]]`, in the sentence that introduces each step. The server reveals a step with the release of the sentence that carries its marker, and the client holds each later step and its sentence until the text shown since the previous step could be read at 238 words per minute, capped per step, with "Show all" to skip the wait. A step appears with a 300 ms ease-out transition; its strokes wipe in by a transformed clip in normal motion; nothing loops, moves once drawn or traces with a dash offset. Earlier elements persist; the model may fade an element to a ghost (a secant replaced by the next one) and rarely erase one. After the reply the student has Previous, Next and Show all over the steps with the step's caption beside them. Under reduced motion the build keeps its order and pacing, the wipe and every transform are dropped, and the step list is open; the end state in both modes is the finished figure with its step list. Rejected: a figure dropped in finished (the dynamic drawing and segmenting evidence), continuous morphs (Tversky; abstract graphs at g = 0.006), revealing at generation speed (it finishes before the first sentence is read), and autoplay replay (full learner control did not help, and the replay's timing would have no source).
4. **The guardrail extends to figures.** The move table says which moves may draw: not on the first practice turn (the reply asks first) nor on a navigation or probe move; yes on naming the representation, the next self-question, naming a rule, discussing the step, the self-explanation question and explaining a lesson. The packet carries the permission as an app-composed field, and a figure on a move that may not draw is dropped by the server. Before submission, and on a lesson section that poses a question, every label, caption and the description run the existing sentence checks, and every numeric channel in "What leaks an answer in a figure" is compared with the key forms the harness already holds; one failing element withholds the whole reply exactly as a leaking sentence does, with its own check name in the audit row. A malformed, oversized, unclosed or second figure is dropped with a fixed one-line notice and the text continues. The prompt tells the model that before an item is checked a figure shows givens and generic illustrations and never the element that carries the answer.
5. **Accessibility is a contract of the renderer.** Every figure carries a description (screened), each step a caption (screened). The SVG is `role="img"` with `aria-labelledby` its title and `aria-describedby` the description and the step list, which sits outside the SVG as an ordered list. The figure wrapper is reachable by Tab so a keyboard user hears the description, and nothing takes focus when a figure or step appears. While the reply streams it is `aria-busy`; when it ends the existing status announcement adds one clause naming the figure and its description. Marks hold 3:1 against the ground in both themes and every role differs from the others with colour removed.
6. **The tutor sees the item's figure.** The packet gains the item figure's kind, window, alt text and, for a table, its cells, under the same allow-list as the stem, so a figure drawn about the item can refer to what the student sees.
7. **Style is a small palette with meaning.** Given in `text-secondary` at regular weight; constructed in `accent-base` at bold weight; highlight as a highlighter underlay in an accent tint behind a bold `accent-base` stroke; error in `state-incorrect`, dashed, with a word label; a ghost in `text-muted`, thin and dotted. Fills sit in accent tints with a hairline edge so neighbouring rectangles stay apart. No decoration, no gradient, no colour chosen by the model.
8. **Measure before widening.** Each turn records whether a figure was drawn, dropped with a reason or withheld, and the golden set gains cases that should draw, cases that must not and cases whose figure leaks, scored by the same code the screen runs.

## Rulings that need the operator [uncertain]

- A lesson or item whose stem states the key's value as a given (LSN-CON-01006's prediction states the open circle at (3, 2) and its key is 2). The screen withholds a figure that draws that given, as it withholds a sentence restating it. Provisional: withhold. Settled by: the operator deciding whether stated givens may be drawn.
- Integer keys. A generic sketch that happens to mark the key's integer is withheld. Provisional: compare integers, as math spans are compared. Settled by: the false-positive rate on the golden set and in use.
- The reading rate. 238 words per minute is an adult figure and school-age readers are slower; mathematical text is slower again. Provisional: 238 words per minute with a cap of 6 seconds per step and a "Show all" skip. Settled by: watching the operator's student use it.
- A second hue for constructions. The accent is monochrome by the operator's token values. Provisional: no new hue; roles ride on ink, weight, dash and tint. Settled by: the operator's token file.
- The stroke wipe. Kept in normal motion for the whiteboard brief with no learning evidence of its own. Settled by: the operator after seeing it.
- Figures kept with the turn for 30 days so a reopened conversation can redraw them. Provisional: yes, under the turns' retention row. Settled by: the operator.
- Drawing on the first practice turn. Provisional: no, the first reply asks what was tried. Settled by: the operator.
