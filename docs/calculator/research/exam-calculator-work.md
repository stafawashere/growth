# Calculator Work on the Calculator-Required Parts

Scope. The indexed free-response corpus in `data/frq_records.json` holds 86 parts marked `calculator`, from 22 calculator-required questions (Questions 1 and 2) in 11 administrations: 2013, 2014, 2015, 2018, 2019 and 2021 to 2026. Scoring guidelines are cached for 2019 and 2021 to 2026 (`sg-NN`); the 2013 to 2015 guidelines are read from the first page of the cached sample documents (`samples-NN-qN p.1`); no 2018 scoring guideline is cached, so 2018 parts are cited to the question pages `frq-18 p.2` and `frq-18 p.3`. No multiple-choice item from Section I Part B is indexed as calculator work in this document, because the cached corpus holds no released Part B items with scoring notes. No question stem, figure or rubric passage is reproduced; anchor quotes stay under 25 words.

## The four capabilities and the recording rule [verified]

The CED expects a graphing calculator that can plot a function in an arbitrary viewing window, find zeros (solve equations numerically), numerically calculate a derivative, and numerically calculate a definite integral (ced p.13). For free-response work obtained with one of those capabilities, "students are required to write the setup (e.g., the equation being solved, or the derivative or definite integral being evaluated)" (ced p.13), and any other built-in feature requires the mathematical steps it replaced (ced p.13). The 2025 Section II Part A directions restate both clauses and add that work "must be expressed in standard mathematical notation rather than calculator syntax" (frq-25 p.2). The same directions appear in 2026 (frq-26 p.2). The 2022 Chief Reader report quotes the same direction lines to teachers (cr-22 p.25). Rounding is practice skill 4.E, "Apply appropriate rounding procedures" (ced p.17).

### A. Net amount from a rate

Task: evaluate a definite integral of a given rate function over a stated interval to get a total. Appears in 2013 Q1(b) (samples-13-q1 p.1), 2015 Q1(a) (samples-15-q1 p.1), 2018 Q1(a) and Q2(b) (frq-18 p.2, frq-18 p.3), 2019 Q1(a) (sg-19 p.2) and 2026 Q1(c) (sg-26 p.4). Archetypes BC-QA-99008, BC-QA-06005, BC-QA-99009. Setup shown: yes, the 2019 guideline splits the part into an integral point and an answer point (sg-19 p.2). Answer form: a decimal, sometimes reported in context to a whole number when the quantity is a count (sg-19 p.2, sg-26 p.4).

### B. Initial value plus accumulated change

Task: add a known value to the definite integral of a rate from the known time to the target time. Appears in 2015 Q2(a) (samples-15-q2 p.1), 2018 Q1(b) and Q1(c) (frq-18 p.2), 2021 Q2(c) (sg-21 p.9), 2022 Q2(c) (sg-22 p.8), 2023 Q2(c) (sg-23 p.7), 2024 Q1(c) (sg-24 p.3) and 2024 Q2(c) (sg-24 p.6). Archetypes BC-QA-09005, BC-QA-06005, BC-QA-06006. Setup shown: yes, with separate points for the integral and for using the initial condition (sg-24 p.3, sg-24 p.6); in 2024 Q2(c) an answer with no supporting work earned no points (sg-24 p.6). Answer form: a three-decimal coordinate or amount, which may be negative or require an integral run backward in time (sg-24 p.6).

### C. Average value of a function

Task: divide the definite integral of a given function by the interval length, sometimes then solve for the input where the function equals that average. Appears in 2014 Q1(c) (samples-14-q1 p.1), 2019 Q1(b) and Q2(b) (sg-19 p.2, sg-19 p.3), 2021 Q1(d) (sg-21 p.6), 2022 Q1(b) (sg-22 p.3), 2023 Q1(c) (sg-23 p.3), 2025 Q1(a) (sg-25 p.2) and 2026 Q2(d) (sg-26 p.9). Archetypes BC-QA-08001, BC-QA-06007, BC-QA-08002. Setup shown: yes, an average value formula point then an answer point (sg-22 p.3, sg-23 p.3, sg-25 p.2). Answer form: a three-decimal value with units in context (sg-22 p.3).

### D. Value or sign of a rate or its derivative at a stated time

Task: compute the derivative of a given model at a point and interpret it, or evaluate given rates at a time to decide increase or decrease. Appears in 2013 Q1(a) and Q1(c) (samples-13-q1 p.1), 2014 Q1(b) and Q1(d) (samples-14-q1 p.1), 2015 Q1(b) (samples-15-q1 p.1), 2018 Q2(a) (frq-18 p.3), 2019 Q1(d) (sg-19 p.2), 2022 Q1(c) (sg-22 p.4) and 2023 Q1(d) (sg-23 p.4). Archetypes BC-QA-04001, BC-QA-04005, BC-QA-02013. Setup shown: the value point in 2023 Q1(d) may appear only inside the interpretation (sg-23 p.4); sign decisions carry a reason point rather than a setup point. Answer form: a signed decimal with units per unit time, or a verdict with a reason.

### E. Equation solved numerically

Task: set two expressions equal (or one to a stated value) and solve on the calculator. Appears in 2013 Q2(b) (samples-13-q2 p.1), 2014 Q1(c) and Q1(d) (samples-14-q1 p.1), 2015 Q2(b) and Q2(c) (samples-15-q2 p.1), 2018 Q1(c) (frq-18 p.2), 2021 Q1(d) (sg-21 p.6), 2023 Q2(b) (sg-23 p.6), 2024 Q2(d) (sg-24 p.8), 2025 Q1(b) (sg-25 p.4), 2025 Q2(b) (sg-25 p.7) and 2026 Q2(c) (sg-26 p.8). Setup shown: yes; in 2025 Q1(b) the answer point needs "the correct answer supported by the appropriate equation" (sg-25 p.4), and presenting the value alone earns neither point (sg-25 p.4). Answer form: a three-decimal input value (time, angle or parameter). In 2025 Q2(b) the solutions are intersection angles that become limits of integration; the Chief Reader report notes they can come from the calculator or from algebra (crabbc-25 p.30).

### F. Extremum located by a critical point and justified

Task: set a derivative (often a net rate) to zero, solve numerically, then justify a global maximum or minimum by a candidates test or a sign argument. Appears in 2013 Q1(d) (samples-13-q1 p.1), 2015 Q1(c) (samples-15-q1 p.1), 2018 Q1(d) (frq-18 p.2), 2019 Q1(c) (sg-19 p.2), 2021 Q2(c) (sg-21 p.9), 2022 Q1(d) (sg-22 p.4), 2025 Q1(d) (sg-25 p.5) and 2025 Q2(c) (sg-25 p.8). Archetypes BC-QA-08006, BC-QA-05006, BC-QA-09011. Setup shown: yes, a point for considering the derivative equal to zero that the bare critical value does not earn (sg-25 p.5). The candidates test needs the function evaluated at each candidate, and those evaluations "must be correct to the first digit after the decimal, rounded or truncated" (sg-25 p.5). The 2025 report describes the task as solving for the critical point with the graphing calculator (crabbc-25 p.3) and records apparent calculator entry errors in the candidate values (crabbc-25 p.4). Answer form: a three-decimal location with a justification, plus a table of candidate values.

### G. Parametric or vector motion at an instant

Task: from given velocity components, compute speed, an acceleration vector (numerical derivatives of the components), a tangent slope, or a time at which speed or slope takes a stated value. Appears in 2013 Q2(c) (samples-13-q2 p.1), 2015 Q2(b) and Q2(c) (samples-15-q2 p.1), 2021 Q2(a) (sg-21 p.7), 2022 Q2(a) and Q2(b) (sg-22 p.6, sg-22 p.7), 2023 Q2(a), Q2(b) and Q2(c) (sg-23 p.5, sg-23 p.6, sg-23 p.7) and 2024 Q2(a) (sg-24 p.5). Archetypes BC-QA-09001, BC-QA-09004, BC-QA-09006. Setup shown: yes, a setup-for-speed point then an answer point (sg-24 p.5); for slope the quotient must be communicated (BC-ERR-09004, sg-23 p.7). An unsupported correct acceleration vector earned one of two points in 2023 (sg-23 p.6). Answer form: a three-decimal scalar or an ordered pair of decimals.

### H. Total distance or arc length

Task: evaluate the integral of the square root of the sum of squared velocity components over an interval. Appears in 2015 Q2(d) (samples-15-q2 p.1), 2018 Q2(d) (frq-18 p.3), 2021 Q2(b) (sg-21 p.8), 2022 Q2(d) (sg-22 p.9), 2023 Q2(d) (sg-23 p.8) and 2024 Q2(b) (sg-24 p.6). Archetypes BC-QA-09007, BC-QA-09003. Setup shown: yes, an integral point and an answer point. The treatment of a bare answer changed across years: unsupported answers earned neither point in 2021 and 2023 (sg-21 p.8, sg-23 p.8), while in 2024 the answer point was earned whether or not the integral point was (sg-24 p.6). Answer form: a three-decimal length with units.

### I. Polar area

Task: evaluate one half the integral of the squared radius, or of a difference of squared radii, between angle limits that are given, read from a figure, or found by solving. Appears in 2013 Q2(a) (samples-13-q2 p.1), 2014 Q2(a) (samples-14-q2 p.1), 2019 Q2(a) and Q2(d) (sg-19 p.3), 2025 Q2(b) (sg-25 p.7) and 2026 Q2(a) (sg-26 p.6). Archetypes BC-QA-09012, BC-QA-09013. Setup shown: yes, with separate integrand points; in 2025 the limits and the one-half factor are assessed in the answer point, not the integrand points (sg-25 p.7). Answer form: a three-decimal area.

### J. Polar derivatives and chain rule to time

Task: compute dr/dθ, dx/dθ or the rate of change of a distance between curves at a stated angle, or multiply by dθ/dt to get a rate in time. Appears in 2014 Q2(b), Q2(c) and Q2(d) (samples-14-q2 p.1), 2025 Q2(a), Q2(c) and Q2(d) (sg-25 p.6, sg-25 p.8, sg-25 p.10) and 2026 Q2(b) and Q2(c) (sg-26 p.7, sg-26 p.8). Archetypes BC-QA-09009, BC-QA-09010, BC-QA-99001, BC-QA-99002, BC-QA-99006. Setup shown: yes, but lightly; in 2025 Q2(a) a single point needs the response to "indicate differentiation of r and provide the correct answer" (sg-25 p.7), and the report notes no expression for the derivative is needed in this calculator-active part (crabbc-25 p.30). Answer form: a three-decimal signed rate.

### K. Integral expression or equation written but not evaluated

Task: write a definite integral, or an equation involving integrals, whose value or solution answers the question, without evaluating it. Appears in 2015 Q1(d) (samples-15-q1 p.1), 2018 Q2(c) (frq-18 p.3), 2019 Q2(c) (sg-19 p.3) and 2022 Q1(a) (sg-22 p.2). Setup shown: the setup is the whole answer. No calculator capability is used, but the notation discipline is the same as the setup point elsewhere.

### N. Work inside a calculator question that needs no capability

Riemann sums and difference quotients from tables, a theorem conclusion, a limit, or a sign read from a supplied second derivative. Appears in 2014 Q1(a), 2021 Q1(a) to Q1(c), 2023 Q1(a) and Q1(b), 2024 Q1(a), Q1(b) and Q1(d), 2025 Q1(c), and 2026 Q1(a), Q1(b) and Q1(d) (for example sg-24 p.2 to p.4, sg-23 p.2 to p.3). These count toward the calculator part's time but not toward calculator fluency; an unsupported sum value earns neither sum point (sg-24 p.3, sg-23 p.3).

## The setup-plus-result rule in the scoring guidelines [verified]

In every cached guideline year the calculator parts score the setup and the numerical answer as separate points. The 2019 guideline lists an integral point and an answer point for each of Q1(a) and Q1(b) (sg-19 p.2). The 2022 guideline gives an average value formula point and an answer point, and a correct integral with broken communication to the average earned one of two (sg-22 p.3). The 2023 guideline does the same for the average rate of flow, and a response that equated the bare integral to the average earned neither point (sg-23 p.4). The 2024 guideline names the first point "Setup for speed" before the answer point (sg-24 p.5).

The 2025 guideline makes the independence explicit. For Q1(a), the setup point needs the correct integral with evidence of division by 4, and "P2 is earned for the correct answer, with or without supporting work" (sg-25 p.3). The same wording is used for the polar area answer (sg-25 p.7) and the rate in time (sg-25 p.10), and for 2026 answer points (sg-26 p.4, sg-26 p.7, sg-26 p.9). Incorrect linkage between a correct integral and a correct answer is treated as scratch work (sg-25 p.3). Two limits apply. First, where the question asks for a solved value, the bare value earns nothing, as in 2025 Q1(b) and the critical points in Q1(d) and Q2(c) (sg-25 p.4, sg-25 p.5, sg-25 p.9). Second, earlier years did not always award the answer point alone (sg-21 p.8, sg-23 p.8, sg-24 p.6). From 2023 onward the stems themselves ask the student to show the setup for the calculations: the phrase appears in 2 prompts in 2023, 4 in 2024, 5 in 2025 and 3 in 2026 across the two calculator questions (frq-23 p.3 to p.4, frq-24 p.3 to p.4, frq-25 p.3 to p.4, frq-26 p.3 to p.4), and it does not appear in the cached 2019, 2021 or 2022 calculator question pages (frq-19 p.2 to p.3, frq-21 p.3 to p.4, frq-22 p.3 to p.4).

The Chief Reader reports repeat the rule. The 2024 report says some students seem unaware that they must show "the necessary setup prior to presenting numerical answers" (cr-24 p.28) and that abandoned work should be crossed out, leaving the setup used in the calculator (cr-24 p.28). The 2023 report says the setup entered into the calculator must be shown in standard notation (cr-23 p.29).

## Radian-mode cues [verified]

The 2025 Question 1 stem carries the parenthetical "(Note: Your calculator should be in radian mode.)" (frq-25 p.3), repeated in the scoring guideline (sg-25 p.2). The same parenthetical appears in 2025 Question 2 (frq-25 p.4, sg-25 p.6) and in both 2026 calculator questions (frq-26 p.3, frq-26 p.4, sg-26 p.2, sg-26 p.6). The 2025 and 2026 Part A directions add "Make sure your calculator is in radian mode" (frq-25 p.2, frq-26 p.2). A bounded search of the cached frq pages finds the word in no other year's pages; the 2018 to 2024 question documents in the cache do not carry the Part A directions page, so absence there does not show the directions lacked it.

Before the parenthetical appeared, the scoring guidelines carried a degree-mode note. In 2022 and 2023 it reads that a response "obtained by using a calculator in degree mode does not earn the first point it would have otherwise earned", with later points generally still available (sg-22 p.3, sg-23 p.4, sg-23 p.6); 2021 applies the same treatment to a specific degree-mode value (sg-21 p.6). Degree-mode notes are found in 2021, 2022 and 2023 guidelines (sg-21 p.6 to p.9, sg-22 p.3 to p.5, sg-23 p.4, sg-23 p.6 to p.8) and not in the cached 2024 to 2026 guidelines. The 2022 and 2023 Chief Reader reports tell teachers to keep calculators in radian mode (cr-22 p.4, cr-23 p.29).

## Angle mode in the College Board Desmos [single-source]

Observed in a browser on 2026-09-29: the College Board version of the Desmos graphing calculator at https://www.desmos.com/testing/collegeboard/graphing opened with Radians selected in its Graph Settings, and its settings menu offered Radians and Degrees. The Desmos help articles describe switching by opening Graph Settings and toggling between Radians and Degrees (desmos-help-graph-settings p.1, desmos-help-trigonometry p.1). Whether the Bluebook-embedded instance always opens in radians, or keeps a setting between questions, is not established by any cached source.

## Three decimals, rounding or truncation, and the one-point cap [verified]

The general scoring note on every calculator question says decimal answers "should be accurate to three places after the decimal point" and that "Within each individual free-response question, at most one point is not earned for inappropriate rounding" (sg-25 p.2). Part notes add that a reported answer may be "rounded or truncated" and that an inappropriately rounded answer does not earn the point (sg-25 p.3). Later part notes add the exception "unless an earlier point was not earned due to inappropriate rounding" (sg-25 p.4), which is how the per-question cap is applied. The 2023 and 2024 notes use "correct to three places" rather than "accurate" (sg-23 p.2, sg-24 p.2), and their model answers already list a truncated alternative in parentheses (sg-24 p.5, sg-22 p.3). A 2022 answer rounded to a whole number earned only the setup point (sg-22 p.3). Candidate values in a justification need only the first decimal place (sg-25 p.5). Rounding is practice skill 4.E (ced p.17).

The 2024 report links three-decimal failures on a solved value to "using the TRACE function rather than using the calculator to solve an equation" (cr-24 p.28), and the 2023 report asks for storing as many places as possible for intermediate values (cr-23 p.29).

## Time per item on the calculator parts [verified]

From research/exam/exam-structure.md, Section I Part B is 13 multiple-choice questions in 38 minutes with a calculator required, and Section II Part A is 2 free-response questions in 30 minutes with a calculator required. That file states no per-question figure. The per-question budget in the app is computed from those pairs as `budget_seconds_per_question`, seconds divided by questions (app/assessment/shape.py), and docs/plan/15-lessons.md states the results as 2.92 minutes per Part B question and 15.0 minutes per Section II question. Of the 86 indexed calculator parts, 13 are table, limit or theorem work that uses no calculator capability but still consumes Part A time.

## Implications for a drill catalogue [inferred]

Ranked by the number of indexed calculator parts that need the kind (denominator 86 parts, 22 questions, 11 administrations). Counts describe the indexed years only.

1. Evaluate a definite integral from a typed function and bounds, three decimals, with the integral written in standard notation. Kinds A, B, C, H, I: 34 of 86 parts, in all 22 questions. Desmos documents definite integrals entered with `int`, bounds, integrand and differential (desmos-help-integrals p.1).
2. Average value, integral divided by interval length, as one written expression. Kind C: 8 of 86 parts, 7 of 11 years.
3. Initial value plus integral, including an integral run backward from a later known time. Kind B: 8 of 86 parts, 6 of 11 years.
4. Solve an equation numerically to three decimals by intersection or zero, not by tracing. Kinds E and F: 20 of 86 parts, 15 of 22 questions. Desmos shows intersection points as points of interest (desmos-help-faqs p.1) and states at least five decimal places for them (desmos-help-whats-new p.1).
5. Critical point then candidates table, evaluating an accumulation function at each candidate. Kind F: 8 of 86 parts, 7 of 11 years.
6. Derivative value at a point from a typed model, with prime notation. Kinds D, G, J: 27 of 86 parts, 16 of 22 questions. Desmos documents evaluating a derivative at a point with prime notation (desmos-help-derivatives p.1).
7. Speed and acceleration from velocity components; slope as a quotient. Kind G: 10 of 86 parts, 6 of 11 years.
8. Polar derivative at an angle and chain rule to time. Kind J: 8 of 86 parts, 3 of 11 years.
9. Total distance as the integral of speed. Kind H: 6 of 86 parts, 6 of 11 years.
10. Polar area with limits found by solving radius equal to radius. Kind I: 6 of 86 parts, 5 of 11 years; solved limits in 1 of those 6 (2025 Q2(b)).
11. Angle-mode check before any trigonometric evaluation. Parenthetical present in 4 of the 22 questions (2025 and 2026); degree-mode notes in 2021 to 2023 guidelines.
12. Carry stored values and report three decimals, rounded or truncated, once per answer. Applies to every numerical answer; tied to BC-ERR-99019.
13. Write the integral or equation without evaluating it. Kind K: 4 of 86 parts, 4 of 11 years.

Every drill should score the written setup and the numerical answer separately, since that is how every cached guideline year scores them.
