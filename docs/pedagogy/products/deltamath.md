# DeltaMath

## Who it serves [verified]

DeltaMath is a teacher-assigned practice platform. Teachers create assignments, and students complete them. The company describes itself as serving teachers and schools, individual students, and homeschooling families through a separate "DeltaMath for Home" product (https://www.deltamath.com, accessed 2026-09-29). A third-party review states that content runs from grade 4 through 12 including Calculus, with more than 2,500 premade problem types (https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2023/03/deltamath-practice-math-through.html, accessed 2026-09-29). The help center says "2500+ skills" from lower elementary through 12th grade (https://help.deltamath.com/content-overview?kb_language=en_US, accessed 2026-09-29). A teacher blog places the top of the range at AP Calculus BC (https://www.hoffmath.com/2021/02/useDeltaMath.html, accessed 2026-09-29). Free accounts get standard problem sets. The PLUS and INTEGRAL subscriptions add help videos, custom problems, tests and video assignments (https://www.deltamath.com/faqs/, accessed 2026-09-29).

## Lesson anatomy [uncertain]

DeltaMath has no lesson in the sense of an explanation followed by practice. The unit is a skill, a single problem type, and an assignment is a list of skills. Each skill asks for a "Required" number of correct answers, defaulting to 5 (https://help.deltamath.com/assignment-skill-settings, accessed 2026-09-29). Problems are generated one at a time until the requirement is met. A third-party service describes the common configuration as 3, 4 or 5 correct in a row, where one wrong answer resets the streak (https://finishmymathclass.com/complete-delta-math-guide/, accessed 2026-09-29). That site sells homework completion, so treat its characterisations with caution. The help center lists eight skill formats, among them Standard (typed numeric, expression or equation answers), Guided (multi-step with hints on wrong intermediate steps), Graphing, and Timed (https://help.deltamath.com/content-overview?kb_language=en_US, accessed 2026-09-29). I found no published figure for minutes per skill. Time per skill is unknown.

## How it introduces a concept [single-source]

It does not. The teacher introduces the concept, and DeltaMath supplies practice. A teacher on Common Sense Education said the product "doesn't really teach" and called it a teacher-centered experience (paraphrased from the summary at https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/5095163/teacher-reviews, accessed 2026-09-29; the page itself later returned only site navigation, so I could not read the original text). Paid tiers add instructional videos per problem type, which teachers can show during work or assign directly (https://www.deltamath.com/faqs/, accessed 2026-09-29). Guided skills give scaffolded prompts to build conceptual understanding (https://help.deltamath.com/content-overview?kb_language=en_US, accessed 2026-09-29). Educator Michael Pershan describes using the skill bank as "a quality textbook hidden inside this practice software" during whole-group teaching (https://pershmail.substack.com/p/practice-software-is-struggling, accessed 2026-09-29, paraphrased by fetch tool, so the quoted phrase is the tool's rendering).

## Worked examples and practice [single-source]

Practice is randomized. The same skill gives neighbouring students different numbers and constants (https://www.deltamath.com, accessed 2026-09-29). A third-party source says the Show Example button opens a nearly identical problem with the steps written beside it, and that it also shows the exact notation the grader expects (search summary of https://www.skipdelta.com and finishmymathclass.com pages, accessed 2026-09-29; I could not load a primary help article for it). I found no primary source for a "Get Help" button by that name. Video help exists only on paid tiers, and teachers can hide it with a "DeltaMath Videos: Visible or Hidden" setting (https://help.deltamath.com/assignment-overview-settings-gc, accessed 2026-09-29). Calculus coverage, as listed by an answer-solving site, includes derivative rules, implicit differentiation, integrals with substitution and parts, limits with L'Hopital, separable differential equations, related rates, optimization, volumes, and slope fields (https://www.skipdelta.com/subjects/calculus, accessed 2026-09-29). That is a competitor's list, and I found no official calculus skill index. Whether BC series topics are covered is unknown.

## Response to a wrong answer [verified]

Immediately, the teacher's "Solutions Shown" setting decides what the student sees. Its options are "Yes" (step-by-step solution after every submission), "On incorrect only", "On correct only", and "No" (https://help.deltamath.com/assignment-overview-settings-gc, accessed 2026-09-29; https://help.deltamath.com/unique-test-settings/, accessed 2026-09-29). The company says students get a detailed explanation as soon as they submit (https://www.deltamath.com, accessed 2026-09-29). The solution is of a generated problem, so it uses that student's own values. Students get multiple tries per question, and after finishing they see all their attempts (https://help.deltamath.com/assignment-skill-settings, accessed 2026-09-29). Later effects come from the penalty setting. "No Penalty" leaves progress alone. "0.5 off" requires one extra correct answer per two wrong. "1 off" requires one extra per wrong answer. "Back to Zero" resets progress on any miss, so the correct answers must be consecutive (https://help.deltamath.com/assignment-skill-settings, accessed 2026-09-29). Grades use a high score, so wrong answers change the working score but do not lower the recorded high score (https://help.deltamath.com/data/assignment-grade-calculation, accessed 2026-09-29). A 2021 teacher blog claims step-by-step solutions are not standard and that custom or specifically assigned problems cannot regenerate a fresh similar problem after a miss (https://www.hoffmath.com/2021/02/useDeltaMath.html, accessed 2026-09-29). It conflicts with the help center and may be out of date.

## Adaptation and sequencing [single-source]

DeltaMath for schools has no adaptive model that I could find. Sequencing is the teacher's choice of skills, and per-skill mastery is a threshold on correct answers. Teachers can cap problems ("No limit", "2 times required", "Equal to required"), set weights (default 1, 0 for optional skills), and turn a skill into a timed fluency task (https://help.deltamath.com/assignment-skill-settings, accessed 2026-09-29). Late work earns 50 percent credit before the late deadline and 0 percent after (https://help.deltamath.com/data/assignment-grade-calculation, accessed 2026-09-29). Mixed problem sets support cumulative review, which is the only spacing mechanism I found (https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2023/03/deltamath-practice-math-through.html, accessed 2026-09-29). No prerequisite graph or spaced-repetition algorithm is published. I searched the help center and the main site. The Home product claims personalization, but I could not read its page.

## What it measures [single-source]

Count of correct answers against the requirement, weighted across skills. With Required = 3, one correct answer scores 33 percent. Two skills at 100 and 33 percent with equal weights give 66.5 percent (https://help.deltamath.com/data/assignment-grade-calculation, accessed 2026-09-29). Teachers can see each attempt, submission time, duration, and, on paid tiers, whether the student watched help videos and how long a timed assignment took (https://www.deltamath.com/faqs/, accessed 2026-09-29). Answer correctness is the only outcome. Reasoning is not scored except in geometric proofs and guided skills.

## Interaction patterns and visual design [inferred]

Answers are typed into structured fields, and the grader is described as strict about form. Third-party sources report that equivalent answers can be rejected, such as a decimal for a fraction or an unsimplified radical (search summary of finishmymathclass.com, accessed 2026-09-29). Other formats include interactive graphing, discovery skills for watching transformations, and dropdown-and-text proofs (https://help.deltamath.com/content-overview?kb_language=en_US, accessed 2026-09-29). Extra-time multipliers (1.5x, 2x) exist for tests (https://help.deltamath.com/assignment-overview-settings-gc, accessed 2026-09-29). I did not view a student session, so layout, typography, motion and mobile behaviour are unknown.

## Engineering signals [uncertain]

Each skill is a parameterised generator producing fresh numbers and constants per request (https://www.deltamath.com, accessed 2026-09-29). Generators carry sub-types, which paid teachers can select individually. The skill catalogue is tagged by state standards for 50 states, DC and Canada (https://help.deltamath.com/content-overview?kb_language=en_US, accessed 2026-09-29). Solutions render from the generated values, which implies each generator ships a worked-solution template. That is my inference. No authoring pipeline, equivalence-checking method or open code is published that I could find. Teacher-created problems exist on paid tiers but lose regeneration according to the 2021 blog above.

## What it does badly [single-source]

Trustpilot shows 2.2 of 5 across 20 reviews (80 percent one-star), with complaints about correct answers marked wrong, progress that stalls or adds problems after a miss, and crashes (https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/deltamath.com, accessed 2026-09-29). That sample is small and self-selected. Common Sense teachers, as summarised by search, cited unstructured content, teachers overwhelmed by choice, and fussy answer entry (https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/5095163/teacher-reviews, accessed 2026-09-29). Pershan argues individual-device practice creates management problems such as rapid clicking and screenshot copying (https://pershmail.substack.com/p/practice-software-is-struggling, accessed 2026-09-29). An educator review warns that repeated correct answers do not show deep understanding (https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2023/03/deltamath-practice-math-through.html, accessed 2026-09-29). Efficacy evidence is thin. One small study in South Tangerang found no significant difference between DeltaMath and non-DeltaMath groups (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381621652_Effect_Of_Deltamath_Towards_Students_Learning_Outcome_A_Study_Case_In_South_Tangerang, accessed 2026-09-29, from search summary). I found no What Works Clearinghouse or ESSA-rated study. Common Sense Education has since discontinued edtech reviews and said its existing reviews would be removed in February 2026, so those links may be dead.
