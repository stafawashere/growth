# Khan Academy

## Who it serves [single-source]

Khan Academy is a free nonprofit platform used by self-directed learners and by classrooms where teachers assign work and read mastery reports (https://www.khanacademy.org/math/ap-calculus-bc, accessed 2026-09-29; https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work, accessed 2026-09-29). The AP Calculus BC course carries a College Board disclaimer that says AP is a registered trademark and that the College Board has not reviewed the resource. The course page suggests "Get ready for AP Calculus" as a prerequisite course. The largest published efficacy sample is 350,000 students in grades 3 to 8, so the evidence base is K-8 and not AP Calculus (https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-efficacy-results-november-2024/, accessed 2026-09-29).

## Lesson anatomy [single-source]

The course page lists 211 skills across 10 instructional units, plus Unit 11 (solved exams) and Unit 12 (standards mappings), which have no exercises (https://www.khanacademy.org/math/ap-calculus-bc, accessed 2026-09-29). Each instructional unit is a flat ordered list of skill lessons, interleaved with quizzes, and closed by a unit test. Unit 1 has 6 quizzes and a unit test across roughly 40 skill lessons. Unit 7 has 2 quizzes. The course ends with a course challenge. A lesson page such as integration by parts holds an article, a link to a video, and practice sets of multiple choice problems with "Check" and "Explain" buttons, plus a link to a full exercise (https://www.khanacademy.org/math/ap-calculus-bc/bc-integration-new/bc-6-11/a/integration-by-parts-review, accessed 2026-09-29). I did not measure video length, exercise length or per-lesson minutes. Per-exercise question counts are unknown; the help center says a pre-exercise card states how many questions there are, without giving the number (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/community/posts/360054768891-Update-Simplified-cards-before-and-after-exercises-quizzes-unit-tests-and-course-challenges, seen in search snippet only, accessed 2026-09-29).

## How it introduces a concept [inferred]

The public course page shows only lesson titles. The article I read states the formula first, names it as the reverse product rule, gives one worked example, and moves to practice, which is a rule-first order (https://www.khanacademy.org/math/ap-calculus-bc/bc-integration-new/bc-6-11/a/integration-by-parts-review, accessed 2026-09-29). That page is a review article, so it may not represent the video introductions, which I did not view. Lesson titles such as "Limits intro" and "Accumulation of change" precede procedure titles, but I cannot confirm what each video does. Treat this section as a deduction from one page.

## Worked examples and practice [single-source]

The review article pairs one fully worked example with one multiple choice problem per practice set, and points to an exercise for more. Hints in the exercise engine follow a documented authoring rule: hints appear one at a time and the last hint gives the answer (https://khan-exercises.readthedocs.io/en/latest/exercises/hints.html, accessed 2026-09-29). Skill lessons include "find the error" exercises, for example "Basic derivative rules: find the error" and "Differentiating functions: Find the error" (course page, accessed 2026-09-29). Strategy lessons appear before mixed practice, for example "Strategy in finding limits" and "Differentiating using multiple rules: strategy".

## Response to a wrong answer [single-source]

In a practice exercise a hint button is visible without an attempt, and using a hint marks the question wrong even if the learner then answers correctly. On quizzes and unit tests the hint button appears only after a wrong submission (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/community/posts/115003344211-Use-of-hints, seen in search snippet only, accessed 2026-09-29). Users report that a wrong answer on a unit test gives no step-by-step solution afterwards (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/community/posts/12175192853389-If-you-get-a-question-wrong-you-can-not-view-a-step-by-step-solution, search snippet title only, accessed 2026-09-29).

After an activity the learner sees a summary of which skills leveled up, leveled down or stayed, and may see recommended lessons (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery, accessed 2026-09-29). In a practice exercise with exactly one error, a bonus question is offered. A correct bonus answer erases the earlier mistake and grants Proficient. Skipping it counts as wrong. It is not offered on some long-passage exercises, teacher-fixed question sets, or exercises with too small a bank (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/35916723756685-How-does-a-student-earn-Proficient-on-a-practice-exercise, accessed 2026-09-29). A staff post says that before this change one wrong answer meant no Proficient (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/community/posts/36008745490317-Update-Introducing-bonus-questions, accessed 2026-09-29).

## Adaptation and sequencing [verified]

Two sources agree on the mastery model. Skills sit at Not started, Attempted, Familiar, Proficient or Mastered. An exercise score under 70 percent gives Attempted, 70 to 99 percent gives Familiar, and 100 percent gives Proficient. A mixed-skill assessment can move a skill one level either way. Only a mixed assessment can move Proficient to Mastered, and a miss drops a skill one level (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work, accessed 2026-09-29; https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery, accessed 2026-09-29).

Mastery challenges are the review mechanism. They unlock when a learner has at least 3 Familiar skills, at least 1 Proficient skill, and has not started one in the last 12 hours. Each has 6 questions on 3 skills drawn from the whole course, 2 questions per skill. Two correct raises a level, two wrong lowers it, one of each holds it. A started challenge expires after 12 hours (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360037494231-What-are-Mastery-Challenges, accessed 2026-09-29). Mastery challenges exist only in math courses. The prerequisite graph is not public, and the course page is one fixed linear order. Placement is unknown. I found no published spacing algorithm, so the 12-hour gate and random skill choice are all that is documented.

## What it measures [verified]

The measured unit is the skill, 211 in this course. Course mastery percent is the count of skills currently Proficient or Mastered divided by total skills, so 5 of 20 gives 25 percent. Both levels count fully, and this rule was changed for Classic Khan Academy in a recent update (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/48431088309645-Update-How-mastery-percentage-is-calculated-on-Khan-Academy, accessed 2026-09-29; https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery, accessed 2026-09-29). Older documentation gave mastery points of 50 for Familiar, 80 for Proficient and 100 for Mastered (search snippet, https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360007253831-What-is-self-paced-Mastery, accessed 2026-09-29), which the current percentage rule replaced. Nothing public measures time to answer, hint depth, or confidence.

## Interaction patterns and visual design [inferred]

The lesson article I read renders math in typeset form with multiple choice answers, a Check button, and an Explain button. Skills are listed as a vertical column with quiz and unit test markers and a five-color level legend on the course page (course page, accessed 2026-09-29). Bonus questions and cards before and after activities are documented. I did not test manipulables, mobile layout or motion, so anything further is unknown.

## Engineering signals [single-source]

Exercises are authored in the Perseus format. The open source repository holds an editor and renderer that "present" a problem, allow interaction and grade the work, as a TypeScript monorepo with Storybook (https://github.com/Khan/perseus, accessed 2026-09-29, from search summary). The older Khan Exercises documentation shows hints authored as ordered child blocks with the last hint holding the answer (https://khan-exercises.readthedocs.io/en/latest/exercises/hints.html, accessed 2026-09-29). Question banks are finite, and bonus questions require enough spare items (bonus question help article above). Mastery level transitions are explicit rules and not a fitted model, based on the help pages. Khanmigo is a GPT-4 based tutor priced at $4 per month that "avoids giving direct answers" and uses Socratic questioning (https://www.khanmigo.ai/learners, accessed 2026-09-29, paraphrased from fetch summary).

## What it does badly [single-source]

Dan Meyer, in 2013, cited a Khan engineer saying that "some students made progress in exercises without necessarily demonstrating understanding" (https://blog.mrmeyer.com/2013/pattern-matching-in-khan-academy/, accessed 2026-09-29). A teacher post on the bonus question feature reports a student who restarted an exercise 9 times to reach 100 percent, and another who guesses, so that a course challenge can move 10 skills up and 20 down (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/community/posts/36008745490317-Update-Introducing-bonus-questions, accessed 2026-09-29). Users ask for a way to skip on mastery challenges, and request unit test restarts. Efficacy evidence is correlational for dosage: about 9 percent of the 350,000 students (about 33,000) reached 30 minutes per week, with an effect size of .36 reported, and Khan calls the design quasi-experimental (https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-efficacy-results-november-2024/, accessed 2026-09-29). This is a self-reported vendor analysis, not independent, and not about calculus. Khanmigo's error rate is unknown; the learner page does not address it.
