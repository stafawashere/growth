# AP Classroom, the daily practice set

Two College Board documents carry most of the detail below, the Teacher Guide dated 2020-04-16 and the FAQ dated 2020-04-23, and the Course at a Glance from the fall 2019 course description. All three predate the current Bluebook-style assessments, so counts and screens may have changed.

## 1. What decides the next problem [verified]

The teacher decides, and nothing in the platform chooses for the student. Topic Questions are listed beside each topic and skill in a unit outline, and the teacher previews them and adds them to Question Bank quizzes (https://media.academicmerit.com/apclassroom/AP%20Teacher%20Guide%20to%20AP%20Classroom.pdf, accessed 2026-09-29). A Personal Progress Check (PPC) is a fixed full form per unit, assigned by the teacher. AP Daily videos are short teacher-led lessons that students can watch, and the student site calls them 15 minutes for most courses in a search summary of https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/ap-daily (page returned 403), while a third-party prep site says 5 to 12 minutes, so length is unresolved.

Independent teacher accounts agree that assignment drives what students see. A calculus and statistics teacher says a unit assigned before its material is taught confuses students (https://saratogafalcon.org/10560/news/ap-classroom-receives-mixed-reactions-teachers/, accessed 2026-09-29). The 2020 FAQ says teachers can reassign PPCs, and that unit guides are advisory because "instructional decisions belong to teachers" in its words (https://media.academicmerit.com/apclassroom/faqs/FAQ.pdf, accessed 2026-09-29). Whether a student can self-select Topic Questions is unresolved, since a third-party site says students cannot (https://fiveable.me/ap-classroom, accessed 2026-09-29) and I found no College Board page saying either way.

## 2. How a set is sized and ordered [single-source]

Sizes are fixed by the College Board. The FAQ says PPCs are full forms of a unit, designed to fit a 45-minute period per part, and cannot be customized, and that there are typically three Topic Questions per topic (FAQ above). Each PPC has a multiple-choice section and a free-response section, and long units split into parts. The 2019 Course at a Glance lists BC counts of about 45 multiple-choice questions in PPC 1, 30 in PPC 2, 15 in PPC 3, 15 in PPC 4, 35 in PPC 5, 30 in PPC 8, 25 in PPC 9 and 45 in PPC 10, with 3 free-response questions in each of those and partial versions in PPC 1 and 2 (https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-calculus-ab-bc-course-at-a-glance.pdf, accessed 2026-09-29). Counts for PPC 6 and 7 did not extract cleanly and are unknown.

Order follows the unit sequence, and a teacher can scramble question order per class, set a time limit with an on-screen countdown, and schedule unlock and due times (Teacher Guide and FAQ above). Students can continue after the timer ends unless the teacher locks the assessment. The Question Bank held more than 15,000 questions in 2020 (Teacher Guide).

## 3. How difficulty is chosen and estimated [single-source]

There is no per-student estimate. The FAQ says PPC questions are new formative items and that early-unit questions are not at the rigor students meet on the exam in May, so difficulty rises by unit design (FAQ above). Topic Questions carry a "Formative" filter in the Question Bank, and no item difficulty statistic is shown to students or teachers in the guides I read. How items are calibrated is not public.

## 4. What happens after a wrong answer [single-source]

Nothing adaptive. Every multiple-choice question has a rationale for each correct and incorrect choice, and free-response questions carry scoring guidelines (FAQ above). The FAQ says teachers see rationales when reviewing responses and a count of students who chose each option. Students see multiple-choice results only if the teacher turns that on, and then also see free-response scores and personal feedback the teacher enters (Teacher Guide, accessed 2026-09-29). In the 2020 optional practice, students could answer a question only once unless the teacher reassigns it (FAQ). A reassigned PPC is a new assessment with identical questions, allowed after the whole class completes it. There is no second try, hint or branch. A teacher blog adds that students get no notification when feedback arrives (https://fysicsfool.com/2022/06/22/pros-and-cons-of-ap-classroom/, accessed 2026-09-29).

## 5. How review is compressed or deduplicated [inferred]

It is not. Teachers assemble their own review from the Question Bank, and the FAQ title "How can I tell if I've already used a question in an assessment?" shows repeat control is manual. Reassigning repeats the identical questions. The Course at a Glance says big ideas and practices spiral across units, which is a curriculum property and not a selection mechanism (Course at a Glance above).

## 6. What the student sees on the day screen [single-source]

The unit outline is the home. It lists topics and skills per unit with Topic Questions links, and assigned work appears when the student signs in (Teacher Guide above). Each student has a personal version of the Progress Dashboard, and teachers see a color-coded Course Summary from bright green for high scores to amber for lower, with class average points per unit and drill-down to single students (Teacher Guide). Free-response results show bars over score bands. A prep-site source says the student dashboard splits results by skill category and compares with national performance, which I could not confirm on a College Board page (fiveable.me above). Practice exam sections use the Bluebook-style interface (https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/instructional-resources/ap-classroom, accessed 2026-09-29).

## 7. What is public about the engineering [uncertain]

Only user guides, FAQs and course descriptions. The College Board does not publish item calibration, form assembly or any adaptive logic for AP Classroom. Secure practice-exam multiple-choice sections require the LockDown Browser (FAQ above). I found no paper, code or talk.

## 8. What it does badly [verified]

Two independent teachers report structure friction. Physics teacher Marc Reif says alignment and accuracy mistakes went uncorrected for three years, long sets cannot be subdivided and printing is poor (fysicsfool link above). The Saratoga Falcon quotes a calculus teacher on the confusion of assigning units before they are taught and on a topic order that differs from textbooks, and notes the 2019 rollout lacked teacher training (Saratoga link above). A competitor, UWorld, says the bank lacks detailed explanations, which conflicts with the FAQ's statement that rationales exist for the new formative items, and the FAQ says summative practice-exam items have none (UWorld, https://collegereadiness.uworld.com/blog/how-to-make-ap-classroom-work-for-teachers/, accessed 2026-09-29).

## 9. What the progress-check design says about the exam's view of daily practice [inferred]

Practice here is a checkpoint per unit, not a daily habit. Each PPC is formative, and the guide says results cannot be used for grades or teacher evaluation, on pain of losing authorization (Teacher Guide above). Each part is sized to a class period, questions come in the exam's own two forms, and free-response items are partial early in the course and complete later. Nothing is adaptive, personalized or spaced, and I found no adaptive element anywhere in the guides. The College Board treats a unit as the smallest planned unit of measurement and a topic as the smallest planned unit of practice, at about three questions.
