# Synthesis

This file reads across `docs/pedagogy/today/products/*.md`, `science.md` and `question-standards.md`. A technique is listed when at least two products use it or one product uses it and the science file carries evidence for it. Fit is judged against the rules that stand whatever the research says: Today is never a schedule, quota, timer or target shown to the student; the session ends when the assembled blocks are empty; only deterministic grading feeds the mastery model; no praise, no study advice, no prediction talk; gates are strengthened, never loosened. Product claims carry the tags the product files give them, mostly [single-source], because vendors describe themselves.

## How the products divide [inferred]

| Group | Products | What decides the next problem | What Growth takes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model-scheduled review | Anki with FSRS, Duolingo (HLR then Birdbrain), Math Academy (FIRe) | a fitted forgetting model over every item or topic ever learned, served in order of predicted forgetting | review by predicted retrievability from the first success, not from the mastery declaration |
| Knowledge-space tutors | ALEKS, Math Academy, Khan Academy | a prerequisite structure that offers the outer fringe, with the student choosing among ready topics (ALEKS carousel, Math Academy dashboard) or a fixed course order (Khan) | the fringe Growth already has, plus the observation that all three let the student pick among equally ready tasks |
| Rating-adaptive practice | Alcumus, IXL, Duolingo Birdbrain | an Elo-style or logistic rating per topic that picks harder problems as the rating rises and easier after misses | nothing live yet; a candidate arm in the simulator, because the science file finds the 85 percent rule out of scope for this learner class and five-term lost |
| Teacher-assigned banks | DeltaMath, AP Classroom, IXL skill practice | the teacher or the student picks the skill, a random draw picks the problem, a required-correct count or a score ends it | the required-correct idea is Growth's mastery rule; nothing adaptive to take |
| Puzzle feeds | Brilliant daily challenge | undocumented; the vendor states a next-problem predictor with no published model | nothing |

No product in the set publishes a learning-outcome study of its next-problem selector. Anki's FSRS is benchmarked on recall prediction (log loss 0.3401 on 349,923,850 reviews), ALEKS's knowledge-check redesign is measured on assessment length (13.3 against 19.6 questions) with a topic gain the authors themselves call confounded by the 2020 cohort, Duolingo reports engagement, and Math Academy publishes an outline of FIRe with no data. The ranking below therefore leans on the science file, and product practice is read as existence proof.

## What the products do badly, read as warnings [single-source]

- A hidden backlog under a daily cap (Anki's 200 review cap, the catch-up guide). Growth: block 1 stops at 5 items or 5 minutes and the rest of the due queue waits; the home screen shows the whole due count, which is the honest half.
- Reviews grouped by topic and announced in advance harm transfer (Matuschak on Math Academy). Growth: the focus cards on Today name the skills of each block before the set starts; the gap analysis records this.
- Points lost on a wrong answer (IXL Challenge Zone 3 to 8 points, ALEKS, DeltaMath Back to Zero, Alcumus un-pass). Growth: no score shown; a failure moves `f_k` and nothing the student reads as a penalty.
- A queue that feels like a database table (Matuschak). Growth: the block titles and intents on Today say why each item is there.
- Strict answer formats (ALEKS, DeltaMath, Alcumus). Growth: CAS equivalence.
- Long unskippable remedial runs after one miss (Math Academy diagnostic). Growth: the requeue is one item at a 1 to 2 day gap, and un-mastery is lossless.
- Engagement measured instead of learning (Duolingo streaks, Brilliant's 6x claim). Growth: the metrics in plan 10 are mastery per hour and delayed accuracy.

## Techniques rejected under the ground rules [inferred]

Recorded in full in `rulings-for-operator.md`: daily XP targets and streaks (Math Academy, Duolingo, Brilliant), leagues and leaderboards (Math Academy, Duolingo), keys and energy limits (Brilliant), SmartScore-style penalties (IXL), the 12-hour gate as a visible timer (Khan), a required-correct count shown as a goal (DeltaMath), a predicted level or score (IXL, Alcumus rating), and the choice to drop an item from the queue (Karpicke).

## What the selection study says about where the gain is [inferred]

The 2026-09-27 study found two-term nearly random because due coverage fires on 1.1 to 7.5 percent of block 2 choices, and the forgetting oracle far ahead on delayed mastery per item. Re-measured on today's engine with `tools/today_metrics.py` (20 synthetic students, fixed world, exponential curve, 60 days), due coverage now decides 33 percent of block 2 choices (16 percent a single winner, 18 percent a tie among the best) because the 2026-09-29 mastery corrections raised skills mastered from practice from 13.6 to 54.8 per student; 67 percent of block 2 picks are still uniform random, the window changes the pick on 13 percent of choices, and a cold-start skill takes a median of 25 items loading it before it is declared. The reading across the evidence:

1. The due test's unit is wrong, not its direction. Every scheduler in the product set and the science file spaces from the first success. Growth's `R_k` exists from the first credited observation and is read only after the six-condition declaration. Ranking every candidate by the retrievability of the observed skills it reaches, in block 2 and in block 3, is the engine-side form of the oracle and costs no new state. It is candidate 1.
2. Block 3 is where the oracle's gain lives. The pool already admits a skill after one unaided success; it is drawn by due coverage and the window, so the least-retained learned skill is served no sooner than any other. Candidate `review_first` isolates that block.
3. The simulator cannot reward lag, so a spacing policy can only show a gain through which skill it reviews, not when. Every number stays a simulation on invented students; a candidate replaces two-term only on the paired mean difference with its interval wholly above 0 on delayed mastery per item and retention at day 30, under both curves, with no loss on skills learned.
4. Difficulty targeting and interleaving-aware ordering are tested for completeness and are not expected to win on this world, since the world models neither difficulty nor contrast.
5. Mastery stays slow by design (rank 9) and no gate moves; the lever is scheduling of learned skills, which needs no mastery.
