# AP Calculus BC Calculator Policy

Which parts require a calculator and which forbid one is stated in [exam-structure.md](exam-structure.md).

## The four required capabilities [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.8 (PDF p.13).

The CED states that a graphing calculator appropriate for use on the AP Exam is expected to have four built-in capabilities:

1. Plot the graph of a function within an arbitrary viewing window.
2. Find the zeros of functions, that is, solve equations numerically.
3. Numerically calculate the derivative of a function.
4. Numerically calculate the value of a definite integral.

These are stated as built-in capabilities of the device, not as procedures a student supplies. The CED frames the graphing calculator as "an integral part of the AP Calculus courses" and notes it is required on some portions of the exam.

## The setup-plus-result rule [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.8 (PDF p.13).

The CED sets a single rule governing how calculator work must be recorded on free-response questions. When a result is obtained using one of the four required capabilities, the response must show the setup that leads to the solution together with the result produced by the calculator. The CED gives the equation being solved, or the derivative or definite integral being evaluated, as examples of a setup.

A second clause covers everything outside the four capabilities: use of other built-in calculator features or programs requires the mathematical steps necessary to produce the results. The distinction is that the four listed capabilities may be invoked as black boxes provided the setup is written, while any other feature must be accompanied by the mathematics it replaced.

This rule is visible in the rubric structure. The 2025 scoring guidelines for the calculator-required question split a two-point part into a setup point and an answer point, with the notes stating that the setup point is earned "for the correct integral, with or without the differential, along with evidence of division by 4" and that the answer point is earned "for the correct answer, with or without supporting work" (BC-SRC-sg-25 p.3). Scoring conventions are collected in [scoring-system.md](scoring-system.md).

## Approved models and the handheld limit [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-web-calc-policy p.1; BC-SRC-web-calc-policy-central p.1; BC-SRC-ced p.35 (PDF p.40).

College Board maintains the list of approved graphing calculator models on its calculator policy page rather than in the CED. The CED points to that page from the Available Resources column of its topic pages, under the label "AP Calculator Policy". The student and AP Central versions of the page are both cached, so the list is recorded here as of 2026-09-29. On both pages, models with the expected built-in capabilities for AP Calculus are marked with an asterisk, and "Only approved graphing calculators from the list are permitted for AP Calculus Exams." The student page adds "This list will be updated as necessary to include new approved calculators", so the cached list is a dated snapshot.

The built-in Desmos sits outside the handheld count. The student page reads "In addition to the Desmos built-in calculator through Bluebook, you can bring up to 2 permitted handheld calculators", and the AP Central page uses the same construction for students.

## Built-in Desmos in Bluebook [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-web-calc-policy p.1; BC-SRC-web-calc-policy-central p.1; BC-SRC-web-bc-students; BC-SRC-desmos-cb-calculators-pdf p.1; BC-SRC-desmos-help-testing-calculators p.1; BC-SRC-desmos-help-assessment-faq p.1.

The Bluebook application used for the digital administration includes a built-in Desmos calculator, and for Calculus BC the policy table names it as the "Built-in Desmos graphing calculator through Bluebook". Its availability follows the part boundaries exactly. Both policy pages say that for Calculus AB, Calculus BC and Precalculus "Desmos calculators will only be available in the calculator-required parts of the exam", so it is not available on the parts where a calculator is not permitted. Only the Bluebook version counts on exam day, because "Only the built-in Desmos calculator through Bluebook can be used during an AP Exam, not the web-based or app-based calculator" (BC-SRC-web-calc-policy p.1).

What the Bluebook graphing calculator changes from desmos.com rests on a single source, Desmos's own College Board PDF, and is a Desmos statement rather than a College Board one. The PDF is marked "Updated for SY2026-2027" and says the exam calculators "may differ from what's available for free on desmos.com". For the graphing calculator it lists two differences. Images, folders, and notes are disabled, and the calculator "automatically checks Log mode" for applicable exponential, logarithmic and power regressions (BC-SRC-desmos-cb-calculators-pdf p.1). Desmos says more generally that testing calculators may differ from those on desmos.com and that its Testing page carries the calculators configured for each exam (BC-SRC-desmos-help-testing-calculators p.1, BC-SRC-desmos-help-assessment-faq p.1). Desmos also states that "Desmos is not considered a computer algebra system (CAS)" (BC-SRC-desmos-help-assessment-faq p.1).

## The four capabilities in the Bluebook calculator [inferred]

Source: BC-SRC-web-calc-policy p.1; BC-SRC-desmos-cb-calculators-pdf p.1; BC-SRC-desmos-help-integrals p.1; BC-SRC-desmos-help-derivatives p.1; BC-SRC-desmos-help-graph-settings p.1; BC-SRC-desmos-help-faqs p.1.

No cached sentence states that the Bluebook graphing calculator has the four required capabilities. It rests on four steps. Calculus BC gets the Desmos graphing calculator. The College Board PDF for SY2026-2027 lists its only differences from desmos.com as the disabled items and the regression Log mode. On desmos.com a definite integral evaluates to a number, prime notation evaluates a derivative at a point, the viewport bounds can be set manually, and points of interest give the coordinates of intercepts and intersections. None of those features is on the list of differences, so they are present. The weakest link is the last step, which reads the PDF's list as exhaustive although the PDF never says "only". Zeros are found through points of interest on a graph, not through a solve command, and whether that meets the CED's "solve equations numerically" is a reading no cached page makes.

## The no-calculator parts [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.198 (PDF p.203); BC-SRC-ced p.29 (PDF p.34).

On Section I Part A and Section II Part B a graphing calculator is not permitted. The CED restates the arrangement from the instructional side in its Unit 1 opener: two sections of the exam do not allow calculator use, and some questions on the other two sections require it. No cached source states a separate answer-recording rule for the no-calculator parts; the setup-plus-result rule is written against results obtained from calculator capabilities, so it has no application where no calculator is available.

## Rounding and reporting [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-sg-25 p.2; BC-SRC-ced p.12 (PDF p.17).

Decimal approximations reported on free-response questions must be accurate to three places after the decimal point, and within each individual free-response question at most one point is not earned for inappropriate rounding. Rounding is also a named practice skill, BC-MPS-4E, "Apply appropriate rounding procedures". The scoring guidelines add that a reported answer accurate to three places after the decimal may be either rounded or truncated, and that an inappropriately rounded answer does not earn the point (BC-SRC-sg-25 p.3).
