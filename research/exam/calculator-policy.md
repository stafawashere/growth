# AP Calculus BC Calculator Policy

Which parts require a calculator and which forbid one is stated in [exam-structure.md](exam-structure.md).

## The four required capabilities [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.8 (PDF p.13).

The CED states that a graphing calculator appropriate for use on the AP Exam is expected to have four built-in capabilities:

1. Plot the graph of a function within an arbitrary viewing window.
2. Find the zeros of functions, that is, solve equations numerically.
3. Numerically calculate the derivative of a function.
4. Numerically calculate the value of a definite integral.

These are stated as built-in capabilities of the device, not as procedures a student supplies. The CED frames the graphing calculator as "an integral part of the AP Calculus courses" and notes it is required on some portions of the exam.

## The setup-plus-result rule [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.8 (PDF p.13).

The CED sets a single rule governing how calculator work must be recorded on free-response questions. When a result is obtained using one of the four required capabilities, the response must show the setup that leads to the solution together with the result produced by the calculator. The CED gives the equation being solved, or the derivative or definite integral being evaluated, as examples of a setup.

A second clause covers everything outside the four capabilities: use of other built-in calculator features or programs requires the mathematical steps necessary to produce the results. The distinction is that the four listed capabilities may be invoked as black boxes provided the setup is written, while any other feature must be accompanied by the mathematics it replaced.

This rule is visible in the rubric structure. The 2025 scoring guidelines for the calculator-required question split a two-point part into a setup point and an answer point, with the notes stating that the setup point is earned "for the correct integral, with or without the differential, along with evidence of division by 4" and that the answer point is earned "for the correct answer, with or without supporting work" (BC-SRC-sg-25 p.3). Scoring conventions are collected in [scoring-system.md](scoring-system.md).

## Approved models and the handheld limit [single-source]

Source: BC-SRC-web-calc-policy; BC-SRC-ced p.35 (PDF p.40).

College Board maintains the list of approved graphing calculator models on its calculator policy page rather than in the CED. The CED points to that page from the Available Resources column of its topic pages, under the label "AP Calculator Policy". A student may bring up to two handheld graphing calculators to the exam. Neither the model list nor the two-device allowance is stated anywhere in the cached CED text, so both rest on the web policy page.

## Built-in Desmos in Bluebook [single-source]

Source: BC-SRC-web-calc-policy; BC-SRC-web-bc-students.

The Bluebook application used for the digital administration includes a built-in Desmos graphing calculator. Its availability follows the part boundaries exactly: it is permitted only on the parts that require a calculator, and it is not available on the parts where a calculator is not permitted. The built-in tool is an alternative to a handheld device on those parts and does not change the two-handheld allowance.

## The no-calculator parts [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.198 (PDF p.203); BC-SRC-ced p.29 (PDF p.34).

On Section I Part A and Section II Part B a graphing calculator is not permitted. The CED restates the arrangement from the instructional side in its Unit 1 opener: two sections of the exam do not allow calculator use, and some questions on the other two sections require it. No cached source states a separate answer-recording rule for the no-calculator parts; the setup-plus-result rule is written against results obtained from calculator capabilities, so it has no application where no calculator is available.

## Rounding and reporting [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-sg-25 p.2; BC-SRC-ced p.12 (PDF p.17).

Decimal approximations reported on free-response questions must be accurate to three places after the decimal point, and within each individual free-response question at most one point is not earned for inappropriate rounding. Rounding is also a named practice skill, BC-MPS-4E, "Apply appropriate rounding procedures". The scoring guidelines add that a reported answer accurate to three places after the decimal may be either rounded or truncated, and that an inappropriately rounded answer does not earn the point (BC-SRC-sg-25 p.3).

## Unresolved [uncertain]

Source: BC-SRC-web-calc-policy.

- The current approved calculator model list is a live web page and was not cached as a PDF in this project. The specific models approved for the 2027 administration are therefore not recorded here.
- Whether the built-in Bluebook Desmos calculator satisfies all four required CED capabilities in the form the CED describes them is not stated by any cached source.
- Whether the two-handheld allowance counts a handheld device plus the built-in Desmos as two devices, or the built-in tool as outside that count, is not established.
- The CED does not define "appropriate rounding" beyond the three-decimal-place standard, and does not state how the one-rounding-point-per-question cap interacts across parts of the same question.
- No cached source states what happens to a response on a calculator-required part that reports a calculator result without a setup beyond the general rubric language.
