# AP Calculus BC Exam Construction

Section layout is in [exam-structure.md](exam-structure.md). Weightings that constrain construction are in [exam-blueprint.md](exam-blueprint.md).

## Where the content scope comes from [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.2 (PDF p.7).

The CED describes the scope of an AP course and exam as derived from an analysis of hundreds of syllabi and course offerings of colleges and universities. A committee of college faculty and expert AP teachers works within the scope of the corresponding college course to articulate what students should know and be able to do. The resulting course framework is described as the heart of the course and exam description and as "a blueprint of the content and skills that can appear on an AP Exam". The phrasing is permissive rather than predictive: the framework bounds the pool, it does not schedule it.

## Who writes the questions and how they are vetted [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.2 (PDF p.7).

The AP Test Development Committees are responsible for developing each exam and for ensuring the questions align to the course framework. The CED characterises development as a multiyear endeavour and lists four stages every exam passes through: review, revision, piloting, and analysis. Three stated goals govern that process: that questions are accurate, fair, and valid, and that there is an appropriate spread of difficulty across the questions.

Committee composition is itself a construction constraint. Members are selected to represent public and private institutions, small and large schools and colleges, and a range of gender, racial or ethnic, and regional groups. Current committee membership is published on AP Central rather than in the CED. Throughout development College Board gathers feedback from secondary and higher education stakeholders.

## The AP Course Audit as an upstream constraint [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.2 (PDF p.7); BC-SRC-ced p.5 (PDF p.10).

Before a school may label a course AP, the AP Course Audit reviews the teacher's syllabus against curricular and resource requirements, with the form submitted by the teacher and the principal or a designated administrator. The CED presents the unit sequence in the publication as optional while the curricular requirements are not. Construction of the exam therefore targets the framework, not any particular sequencing of it.

## How the CED frames its sample questions [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.203 (PDF p.208).

The sample questions section opens with an explicit statement of purpose. The samples "illustrate the relationship between the course framework and the AP Calculus AB and BC Exams and serve as examples of the types of questions that appear on the exams". The framing is illustrative. The CED does not present the samples as a retired form, does not state that they are equated, and does not attach difficulty statistics to them.

The section is organised the same way a live form is. Multiple choice comes first, headed "PART A (AB or BC)" with the note that graphing calculators are not permitted on that part, followed by the calculator part, then free response. The header convention "(AB or BC)" marks material shared with the AB exam and is the same convention used in the scoring guidelines.

## Skill and topic tagging of sample questions [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.216 (PDF p.221).

The CED states that a table follows the sample questions showing which skill, learning objective or objectives, and unit each question relates to, and that the table also provides the answers to the multiple-choice questions. The table has four columns beyond the question number: Answer, Skill, Learning Objective, Unit. Each row carries exactly one skill code and one learning objective code in the cached extraction, for example question 1 tagged answer D, skill 1.E, objective LIM-1.E, Unit 1; question 2 tagged answer B, skill 3.D, objective FUN-2.A, Unit 2.

This is the only place in the CED where individual questions are mapped to the framework, and it establishes the tagging granularity the exam is built against: one skill and one learning objective per multiple-choice question. Free-response tagging is finer. The scoring guidelines printed in the CED attach skill codes to individual scoring points rather than to the question, and a single point may carry more than one, for example a point tagged 3.F and 4.B for an interpretation with units and a point tagged 1.D and 4.C for a definite integral (BC-SRC-ced p.218, PDF p.223).

## Task verbs as a construction vocabulary [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.202 (PDF p.207).

The CED publishes a closed list of task verbs commonly used in free-response questions, each with a definition that fixes what a response must contain. Approximate, Calculate or Write an expression, Determine, Estimate, Evaluate, Explain, Identify or Indicate, Interpret, Interpret when given a representation, Justify, Represent, and Verify. Several definitions carry procedural obligations directly, for instance that Calculate tasks require the expression or equation to be presented to show work, and that Justify requires identifying a logical sequence of definitions, theorems, or tests, explaining why they apply, and then applying them. The CED also records phrasing variants, that Determine tasks may be phrased as Find and Explain tasks as Give a reason for. Verb records live in the taxonomies registry; this section covers only their role in construction.

## The AP Reading [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.3 (PDF p.8); BC-SRC-cr-23 p.1; BC-SRC-cr-24 p.1; BC-SRC-crabbc-25 p.1.

Multiple-choice questions are machine scored. Free-response questions are scored by college faculty and expert AP teachers, most at the annual AP Reading and a small portion online. The CED states that all Readers are thoroughly trained and that their work is monitored throughout the Reading for fairness and consistency, and that in each subject a college faculty member serves as Chief Faculty Consultant, maintaining the accuracy of the scoring standards with the help of Readers in leadership positions.

The Chief Reader reports give the scale of the operation. Readers for Calculus AB and BC combined numbered 1,359 in 2023, 1,572 in 2024, and 2,201 in 2025. Julie Clark of Hollins University is named as Chief Reader in both the 2023 and 2024 reports. Each report states its own purpose: to give an overview of each free-response question and of how students performed, including typical student errors, plus general comments on the skills and content students most often have trouble with.

The reports are written per question and per part. The 2023 report, for example, records for one part that a large proportion of responses recognised that applying a theorem was intended, that many earned the first point by establishing the equality of two function values, that quite a few failed to explain that differentiability gave continuity on the required closed interval, and that a few applied the Extreme Value Theorem in an argument the report calls not valid (BC-SRC-cr-23 p.3). Findings of this kind are catalogued in the scoring and misconception files, not here.

The 2025 report changes format, printing a mean for each of the nine scoring points of a question separately for AB and BC alongside the overall question mean, and labelling each question with both a Task list and a Topic (BC-SRC-crabbc-25 p.2). This gives point-level resolution on construction that the 2023 and 2024 reports do not.

## Copyright constraint on reproduction [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-terms-conditions p.5.

The AP Services Terms and Conditions state that all content available through AP Services, including questions and answers, is protected under United States copyright law and is the sole property of College Board, and that content may not be reproduced, redistributed, modified, or reused without express written permission. This library therefore stores metadata, paraphrase, and short anchor quotes only, and reproduces no question stems, figures, or full rubric text, as set out in the library README.

## Unresolved [uncertain]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.2 (PDF p.7).

- The CED names review, revision, piloting, and analysis as development stages but gives no detail on pretesting: whether unscored pilot items appear on live forms, and if so how many, is not stated in any cached source.
- No cached source describes how a form is assembled against the published unit and practice weighting ranges, or who approves the final assembly.
- The CED does not state whether the published sample questions are retired live items, purpose-written items, or a mixture.
- Whether the one-skill-per-question tagging in the sample table reflects the internal tagging of live items, or is a simplification for publication, is not established.
- Reader training, rubric calibration, and backreading procedures are referenced by the CED only as "thoroughly trained" and "monitored"; no cached source documents the procedures.
- Reader counts are for AB and BC combined; no cached source splits them by exam.
- No Chief Reader is named in the cached 2025 report pages read in this pass.
