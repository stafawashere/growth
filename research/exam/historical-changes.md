# AP Calculus BC Exam Historical Changes

Current section counts and timings are stated once, in [exam-structure.md](exam-structure.md).

## 2019: the framework rewrite [uncertain]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.i (PDF p.2); BC-SRC-web-key-changes.

The cached Course and Exam Description is labelled "Effective Fall 2020" on its title page and carries the standing notice that AP course and exam descriptions are updated periodically. It contains no revision history, no description of a predecessor framework, and no statement of what changed relative to earlier editions.

The framework the CED does describe is organised into big ideas, units, enduring understandings, learning objectives, and essential knowledge statements, with a separate mathematical practices table, and the CED attributes this design to the Understanding by Design model of Wiggins and McTighe (BC-SRC-ced p.13, PDF p.18). That the shift to this design was introduced for the 2019 to 2020 school year is not established by anything in the cache. The College Board page that would carry the change history, BC-SRC-web-key-changes, returned HTTP 403 to a direct fetch on 2026-09-19 and its content was not obtained.

## 2023 onward: the digital transition [single-source]

Source: BC-SRC-web-bc-students.

The exam is now administered in a hybrid digital format. Multiple-choice questions are answered in the Bluebook application. Free-response prompts are viewed in Bluebook while free-response answers are handwritten in paper booklets. The Bluebook environment includes a built-in Desmos graphing calculator whose availability follows the calculator status of each part, as recorded in [calculator-policy.md](calculator-policy.md).

Tagged single-source. This rests on one College Board student-facing web page checked on 2026-09-19. No cached PDF in this corpus describes the delivery format, and the cached CED, which is the Fall 2020 edition, does not mention Bluebook or digital administration anywhere. The year the hybrid format first applied to AP Calculus BC specifically is not established by the cached material.

## Fall 2026: CED clarifications and corrections [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced-clarifications-2026 p.1; BC-SRC-ced-clarifications-2026 p.2.

College Board published a Clarifications and Corrections document for the AP Calculus AB and BC CED, headed "To be implemented for Fall 2026". It records general clarifications, two essential knowledge rewordings, and three exam information changes.

Two essential knowledge statements were updated. The document gives the new text in each case.

BC-EK-FUN-1C1, in Unit 5, was "Updated to read, 'If a function f is continuous over the interval [a, b], then the Extreme Value Theorem guarantees that f has at least one minimum value and at least one maximum value on [a, b].'"

BC-EK-FUN-7B2, in Unit 7, was "Updated to read, 'There may be infinitely many solutions to a differential equation.'"

The interval brackets in both statements are rendered as mathematical glyphs in the source and are reconstructed here as [a, b].

Under Exam Information, the document states that for multiple-choice questions, Part A, "The number of questions was changed from 30 to 29, and the timing was changed from 60 minutes to 62 minutes", and that for multiple-choice questions, Part B, "The number of questions was changed from 15 to 13, and the timing was changed from 45 minutes to 38 minutes". A third exam-information change adds a paragraph about accessible technology available for students who are blind or visually impaired.

The net effect on the multiple-choice section is 42 questions in 100 minutes where the prior structure had 45 questions in 105 minutes. The document does not state a change to Section II, to section weightings, or to unit or practice weightings.

Three general clarifications are also recorded: the Available Resources listings on topic pages were updated to indicate whether a resource sits in AP Classroom or the Online Teacher Community, the Progress Dashboard section was replaced by a Reports section, the Question Bank section language was updated, and the Technology Needs and Use of Graphing Calculators sections were combined and clarified.

## May 2027: first administration under the revised structure [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.198 (PDF p.203); BC-SRC-web-bc-exam; BC-SRC-web-exam-dates.

The May 2027 administration is the first AP Calculus BC exam given under the revised multiple-choice structure. The cached CED exam overview already carries the revised figures, 29 questions in 62 minutes for Section I Part A and 13 questions in 38 minutes for Part B, matching the Fall 2026 clarifications and confirmed against the College Board exam page on 2026-09-19. Section II is unchanged at 6 questions, 2 calculator-required in 30 minutes and 4 no-calculator in 60 minutes. Total testing time remains 3 hours and 10 minutes. The exam is scheduled for Monday, May 10, 2027, Session 1 [single-source, BC-SRC-web-exam-dates].

Because the revised counts took effect from the Fall 2026 clarifications, the score distributions and scoring statistics in [scoring-system.md](scoring-system.md), which cover 2023 to 2025, describe forms built under the 45-question multiple-choice structure.
