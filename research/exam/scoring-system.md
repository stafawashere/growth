# AP Calculus BC Scoring System

Section and question counts are in [exam-structure.md](exam-structure.md). What the AB subscore covers is in [exam-blueprint.md](exam-blueprint.md).

## How a raw score becomes a reported score [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.3 (PDF p.8); BC-SRC-web-score-setting; BC-SRC-web-equating.

The CED describes the pipeline in one sentence: multiple-choice questions are scored by machine, free-response questions are scored by college faculty and AP teachers at the annual AP Reading with a small portion scored online, and then "Scores on the free-response questions and performance assessments are weighted and combined with the results of the computer-scored multiple-choice questions, and this raw score is converted into a composite AP score on a 1-5 scale."

The multiple-choice score is the number of questions answered correctly, with no deduction for incorrect answers, so there is no guessing penalty [single-source, BC-SRC-web-score-setting]. The conversion from composite to the 1 to 5 scale is set by equating, which places each year's form on the scale established for prior forms [single-source, BC-SRC-web-equating].

The CED states that AP Exams are criterion referenced rather than norm referenced or curved, so every student meeting the criteria for a 2, 3, 4, or 5 receives that score regardless of how many students do. The criteria named are the number of points college students earn on AP questions administered to them, performance predictive of success in a subsequent college course, and the number of points college faculty judge necessary for each level (BC-SRC-ced p.3, PDF p.8).

## General scoring conventions [verified]

Source: BC-SRC-sg-25 p.2; BC-SRC-sg-25 p.3.

The General Scoring Notes that head each 2025 scoring guideline state three conventions. The model solution is presented in standard mathematical notation. Answers, numeric or algebraic, "need not be simplified". Decimal approximations "should be accurate to three places after the decimal point", and "Within each individual free-response question, at most one point is not earned for inappropriate rounding."

The per-part scoring notes add two conventions about supporting work. A point may be specified as earned "with or without supporting work", as for the answer point on 2025 Question 1 part A, which means a correct final value earns the point on its own. Separately, "Incorrect or unclear communication between the correct integral and the correct answer is treated as scratch work and is not considered in scoring", so a faulty equality chain between a correct setup and a correct answer does not by itself cost either point. The same notes state that a reported answer may be "rounded or truncated" to three places and that an inappropriately rounded answer does not earn the point.

Taken together these define a rubric that is point-by-point rather than holistic. Each point has its own earning condition, and the conditions are stated independently of one another. Derived point types are registered in the scoring files rather than here.

## What is not published [inferred]

Source: BC-SRC-ced p.3 (PDF p.8); BC-SRC-web-score-setting.

The CED says free-response scores are "weighted and combined" with multiple-choice results but gives no weights. No cached or web source in this project states the raw point weight applied to the multiple-choice section relative to the free-response section, the composite scale maximum, or the composite cut scores that separate 1 from 2, 2 from 3, 3 from 4, and 4 from 5. The same holds for the AB subscore composite. These are treated as not public, and any weighting figure appearing elsewhere in this library is an inference rather than a published value.

Item-level publication is uneven. The scoring statistics files give per-question means and standard deviations for all three years. The 2025 Chief Reader report goes further and prints a mean for each of the nine individual scoring points on each question, separately for AB and BC, for example BC1 point 5 at 0.84 and BC1 point 8 at 0.18 against an overall BC1 mean of 5.22 (BC-SRC-crabbc-25 p.2). The 2023 and 2024 Chief Reader reports print only whole-question means (BC-SRC-cr-23 p.2; BC-SRC-cr-24 p.2). Nothing is published about multiple-choice item difficulty, and no cached source gives the raw-score-to-composite mapping for any year.

## Unresolved [uncertain]

Source: BC-SRC-web-score-setting.

- Raw point weighting of multiple choice against free response is not published. Tagged inferred wherever this library needs it.
- Composite cut scores for 1 to 5 are not published for any year in the corpus.
- The no-guessing-penalty rule and the equating description rest on single web sources checked on 2026-09-19, with no cached PDF corroboration.
- How the AB subscore composite is assembled from its contributing items, and whether it is equated separately from the BC composite, is not established.
- The 2024 Chief Reader report reproduces the same BC and AB subscore figures as BC-SRC-dist-24 and BC-SRC-absub-24 (BC-SRC-cr-24 p.1), so all three years are corroborated; no year has independent non-College-Board corroboration.
- Per-point means exist only for 2025. Whether College Board will continue printing them, and whether comparable figures exist for 2023 and 2024 outside the cache, is not established.
- Whether the "at most one point is not earned for inappropriate rounding" cap applies per question or per part is stated as per question in 2025 but was not checked against earlier scoring guideline years.
