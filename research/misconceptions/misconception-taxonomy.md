# Misconception Taxonomy, AP Calculus BC

A misconception record names a possible internal belief. It is never observed directly. What is observed is an error record, and a single error is usually consistent with more than one misconception, which is why the Rivals column exists. Rows are drawn from `data/misconceptions.json`; the Evidence column gives the per-record tag, and the tag on each H2 is the tag most common among that section's rows. Records retired during the cross-unit consolidation are listed in their own section and are not repeated in the unit tables.

## Concept image and concept definition [single-source]

Tall, D. and Vinner, S. (1981). Concept image and concept definition in mathematics with particular reference to limits and continuity. Educational Studies in Mathematics 12(2), 151 to 169. The distinction is used here as a frame, not as evidence about AP responses; it comes from one source and is tagged accordingly.

In that frame a learner carries a concept image, the whole collection of pictures, examples, and procedures associated with a term, alongside a concept definition, the form of words the learner would recite if asked. The two can disagree without the learner noticing, because everyday work draws on the image and only a direct request draws on the definition. Several registry records are readable this way. BC-MIS-01001 is consistent with a concept image of a limit built entirely from substitution, even where the recited definition mentions approach. BC-MIS-05011 is consistent with an image in which the plotted curve is whatever function is under discussion, so a graph of f prime is read as f. BC-MIS-99008 is consistent with an image of infinity as a large number, which supports arithmetic that the definition of a limit at infinity does not license.

The frame also predicts nothing about scoring and is not used here to do so. Its value in this library is that it separates two probes that look alike: asking a learner to state a definition tests the concept definition, while asking for a judgement in an unfamiliar case tests the concept image, and the `discriminating_probe` field on each misconception record is written for the second kind.

## Errors and misconceptions are different records [inferred]

An error record states what a reader can see on the page. A misconception record states a belief that would account for it. The registry keeps them apart because the mapping is many to many in both directions, and because some errors have no conceptual cause at all.

Worked example one, an error with no necessary misconception. BC-ERR-99006 records a differential omitted from an integral expression. Its `non_conceptual_causes` list transcription haste and the habit of reading the integral sign alone as the instruction, and the Chief Reader citations behind it describe the omission as a notation loss rather than as a failure to know what the integral means. A response can omit dx and then integrate correctly with respect to the right variable, which is exactly the pattern that makes a conceptual diagnosis unsupported. The error is real, the point is lost, and no misconception has to be posited.

Worked example two, an error that is consistent with a specific misconception. Reporting the integral of f prime as f prime again is recorded in the registry as a failure to carry out the antidifferentiation step; it is consistent with BC-MIS-99004, the belief that an accumulation function is the antiderivative outright, and with a confusion of differentiation and antidifferentiation as operations, which the registry expresses through records such as BC-MIS-06019 on antidifferentiation distributing across a product. The two readings are rivals for the same observation, and the `discriminating_probe` on each is what separates them.

The rule this section enforces on the rest of the library is a phrasing rule. An error record says what happened. A misconception record says what the behaviour is consistent with, or what it may indicate. No record says that a student believes something.
