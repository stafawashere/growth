# What Earns a Justification Point

Citations name the cached document and page, so `sg-25:5` is the cached 2025 scoring guidelines at page 5.

## Justify, give a reason, and give reasons [verified]

The guidelines treat these as three different demands. A part saying to justify an answer carries a justification point that requires a global argument (sg-25:5, sg-25:19, sg-23:15). A part saying to give a reason carries a reason point that can be satisfied by one correctly targeted sentence (sg-25:17, sg-26:15, sg-24:4). A part saying to give reasons for answers in the plural carries one point per conclusion, as at sg-26:17 where the absolute minimum with reason and the absolute maximum with reason are separate points.

The reason point is graded on what the sentence refers to rather than on its length. sg-26:8 states a response does not need to cite a derivative test by name, nor restate the critical value, but does need to classify the critical point correctly with analysis using a derivative test.

## Global versus local arguments [verified]

The sharpest recurring rule is that a local argument does not justify a global claim. sg-25:5 states that a response presenting a local argument, such as a First Derivative Test or a Second Derivative Test, or an incorrect global argument, does not earn the justification point but is eligible for the answer point with the correct answer. sg-25:9 repeats the wording for a polar maximum, and sg-25:19 for an absolute minimum on a graph question.

A local argument can be raised to a global one by a uniqueness clause. sg-25:5 accepts an argument that the derivative changes sign from positive to negative at the critical point, followed by the statement that it is the only critical point in the interval, therefore it is the location of the absolute maximum. sg-22:12 states directly that a justification using a local argument must also state that the critical value is the only critical point.

## Sign analysis of a derivative [verified]

The purest global form is a two-sided sign statement. sg-25:5 accepts that the derivative is positive on the left portion of the interval and negative on the right portion, therefore the critical value is the location of the absolute maximum on that interval. sg-25:19 accepts the non-strict form with inequalities that allow equality at the boundary. sg-22:12 accepts the same shape for a minimum.

Sign analysis is also what earns the separate consideration point that precedes a justification. sg-25:5 states that a response discussing the sign of the derivative changing, or using the phrase "critical points of A", earns the consideration point, while presenting only the solved critical value does not. sg-26:17, sg-25:8 and sg-25:19 carry the identical construction.

Nothing in the corpus read requires a drawn sign chart, and nothing in it awards a point for one. Every accepted form in sg-25:5, sg-25:9 and sg-25:19 is written in symbols or words. sg-22:5 allows the candidate values behind a justification to appear only in a candidates table, as limits of integration, or on a number line, which is the closest the guidelines come to accepting a chart, and even there the value is what is read, not the chart.

## The candidates test [verified]

Where the prompt asks for an absolute extremum on a closed interval, the model solution uses a candidates test and the notes state what it must contain. sg-25:5 requires a global argument that correctly evaluates the function at the interior critical point and at both endpoints. sg-25:19 requires evaluations or reasoning for each of the four candidates and no other values. sg-23:15 states that a response presenting any error in evaluating at any critical point or endpoint will not earn the justification point, and that a response that does not consider both endpoints does not earn it.

sg-23:15 also lists the ways a candidate may be dropped without evaluation. A candidate need not be evaluated if it is eliminated because it is the location of a local maximum, or eliminated by reference to an earlier part, or covered by an argument that the derivative is non-negative beyond a point so a later value exceeds an earlier one.

Candidate evaluations are held to a looser precision than reported answers. sg-25:5 and sg-25:9 require the evaluations to be correct to the first digit after the decimal, rounded or truncated. sg-22:5 accepts up to three decimal places and correctly rounded integers.

The candidates test is not universally accepted. sg-26:8 states that a candidates test is not sufficient justification to earn the classification point when the prompt asks for a derivative test.

## Theorem hypotheses [verified]

Existence questions are scored as a hypothesis point plus a conclusion point. The hypothesis point is earned only by deriving continuity rather than asserting it. sg-25:12 states that to earn the point a response must state that the function is continuous because it is differentiable, and that a response simply stating the function is continuous without justification does not earn it. sg-26:5 states the same for a differentiable difference function, and sg-22:14 extends the chain from twice differentiable through differentiable to continuous.

The conclusion point has three named components. sg-26:5 requires the response to indicate that the function is positive at one endpoint and negative at the other, to state that the function is continuous, and to answer yes in some way, and it states that a response of "yes, IVT" is not sufficient. sg-25:12 lists the same three components. sg-26:5 adds that a response that has earned the hypothesis point does not need to restate continuity in the conclusion.

Naming the theorem is optional but must be correct when done. sg-25:12 and sg-26:5 both state that a response need not explicitly name the Intermediate Value Theorem, but if a theorem is named it must be correct. sg-22:14 repeats the sentence.

The two points are only loosely coupled. sg-25:12 and sg-26:5 both state that a response does not need to earn the hypothesis point to be eligible for the conclusion point.

The Mean Value Theorem is scored with the same architecture but a different first point. sg-23:3 makes the first point a statement that the function takes equal values at the two endpoints, or that their difference is zero, and requires the second point to build on the first, to state continuity from differentiability, and to answer yes. sg-23:3 states a response may reference either the Mean Value Theorem or Rolle's Theorem, and that a response referencing the Intermediate Value Theorem cannot earn the conclusion point. sg-21:17 requires differentiability on the open interval and continuity on the closed interval, and states the theorem need not be explicitly stated provided the conditions and the conclusion are stated.

## Reasons tied to the object the prompt names [verified]

Where a question gives the graph of a derivative and asks about the original function, the reason point is earned only by reasoning about the graphed object. sg-25:17 gives the reason point for saying the graphed function changes from increasing to decreasing, for saying its slope changes sign, or for saying it attains a relative extremum there, and withholds it for saying the original function changes concavity or that the second derivative changes sign. sg-26:15 carries the identical list for a graph of the first derivative. sg-22:11 states the same in the older phrasing and adds that reasoning based only on the concavity of the original function is insufficient.

Both documents reject vague subjects. sg-25:17 and sg-26:15 state that a response relying on an ambiguous term such as "the function" or "the graph" does not earn the reason point, and sg-22:11 names "it", "the function" and "the derivative" as vague or undefined terms that cannot carry the point.

The same tying requirement appears for compound conditions. sg-26:16 requires a response to refer to the derivative and to cite both that it is positive and that it is decreasing, and lists two special cases where addressing only one half of the condition earns the interval point but not the reason point.

## Reasons about slope fields and concavity [verified]

sg-26:11 requires an explanation about a slope field to reference the sign of the slopes of the drawn line segments, or what that sign implies for the graph of the solution, and states that referencing the sign of the derivative without connection to the slope field does not earn the point. It then names four communication errors that fail: references to the sign of the slope field, the slope of the slope field, the slope of the differential equation, and the increasing behaviour of the slope field.

For over and underestimate questions the reason must run from the second derivative to concavity to the position of the tangent line. sg-26:12 states a response that says "concave up, so underestimate" earns the reason point if the sign point was earned, and that referencing the concavity of the first or second derivative does not earn it. sg-23:10 states the reason must include that the second derivative is negative, or that the first derivative is decreasing, or that the graph is concave down, as well as the conclusion. Both documents reject an argument based on the second derivative or concavity at a single point (sg-23:10, sg-26:12).

## Justification inside series work [verified]

Series questions carry justification points with their own wording. sg-25:25 states that to earn the final interval point a response must correctly analyse the series at both endpoints and present the correct interval, and that naming an appropriate test is sufficient for the analysis at each endpoint, listing the alternating series test, direct comparison, limit comparison and the integral test as acceptable. sg-24:19 pairs a substitution point with an answer-with-reason point that names the comparison test.

Error bound questions demand an inequality rather than an equality. sg-25:22 states that to earn the analysis point a response must have earned the bound point and must explicitly connect the bound with the target number, and that a response declaring the error is equal to that value does not earn the point. sg-26:22, sg-23:20 and sg-22:21 carry the same rule, and sg-26:22 and sg-22:21 add that the response must state the series is a convergent alternating series whose terms decrease in absolute value to zero.
