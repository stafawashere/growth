# Notation and Communication in the Scoring Guidelines

Citations name the cached document and page, so `sg-25:7` is the cached 2025 scoring guidelines at page 7. The guidelines open every question with the note that the model solution is presented using standard mathematical notation (sg-25:2, sg-26:2), which frames the rest of this file: the model is a model, not a requirement.

## The differential is normally optional [verified]

The recurring phrase is "with or without the differential". sg-25:7 applies it to both polar area integrand points, sg-25:14 to an integrand point, sg-26:4 to an integral point, sg-26:9 to an average value integral, and sg-26:19 to an arc length integral and a volume integral. sg-22:2 goes further and states that a response missing the differential or using the wrong variable letter is eligible to earn the point.

The exception is where the differential carries information the rubric needs downstream. sg-23:8 lists four cases for a missing differential in an accumulation expression, and the four cases differ in whether the response stays eligible for the answer point. sg-22:8 lists the same four-way split for a different question. In both, the pattern is that a bare missing differential is forgiven, while a missing differential combined with a value or an initial condition written on the wrong side changes which of the three points remain available.

## Limit notation [verified]

Improper integrals are the one place the guidelines treat notation as a point in its own right. sg-23:17 states that to earn the point a response must correctly use limit notation throughout the problem and not include arithmetic with infinity, and gives two rejected forms. sg-22:19 places the same requirement on the answer point instead, stating the response must use correct limit notation and cannot include arithmetic with infinity.

sg-26:20 both relaxes and sharpens this. It accepts limit notation applied either to the integral or to an antiderivative of the correct form, lists three acceptable shapes, and then states the point is banked once earned, so subsequently presenting arithmetic with infinity does not affect scoring. It also states that a response presenting only an expression with arithmetic with infinity does not earn the point, because the point is for correctly using limit notation.

Arithmetic with infinity is treated as scratch work elsewhere too. sg-25:4 states that for the end-behaviour value point, arithmetic with infinity will be considered as scratch work and will not be considered in scoring.

## The equal sign [verified]

Two distinct misuses appear.

The first is equating a general expression to a single numerical value. sg-22:6 states a response with an incorrect equation of the form "function equals constant" will not earn the point, and adds that such a response remains eligible for points for similar errors in subsequent parts. sg-23:6 states a response that equates a general derivative expression to a numerical value earns only one of the two acceleration points for that reason.

The second is an equality chain that misstates what is being computed, normally between an integral and a divided or scaled value. sg-25:3 states that incorrect or unclear communication between the correct integral and the correct answer is treated as scratch work and is not considered in scoring, and gives three examples that each keep both points despite the broken chain. sg-26:9 repeats the rule for a polar average value with three parallel examples. sg-25:18 applies it to a label and a value. This is the most consequential notation decision in the corpus read, and it reverses earlier practice: sg-23:4 and sg-22:3 each take one of two points for the same shape, and sg-23:16 withholds an answer point for an area sentence that equates an integral to a negated value.

Where an approximation is intended, the guidelines read the equal sign charitably. sg-25:13 and sg-26:3 both instruct readers to read an equals sign as an approximation sign for the sum form point, and sg-26:22 and sg-22:22 give the same instruction for series work.

## Derivative notation [verified]

Loose derivative notation is generally accepted when the intent is clear. sg-25:7 lists two forms as correct communication and then states that responses with incorrect communication, including a general derivative function equated to a value, a bare primed symbol equated to a value, and a derivative written without an evaluation bar, are nonetheless sufficient to earn the point. sg-24:8 accepts a derivative written in Leibniz form in place of prime notation.

Notation for the variable of integration is not policed. sg-22:5 states a response with incorrect notation involving the two variable letters is eligible to earn all four points of the part.

## Labels [verified]

Labels are scored where the prompt asks for them. sg-25:18 states unlabelled values earn neither of the two value points, then adds that incorrect communication between a label and its answer will be treated as scratch work and will not impact scoring, giving an example where a mislabelled integral still earns its point. sg-22:10 states that unlabelled values are read left to right and top to bottom, and that a single value must be labelled to earn any points.

Vectors follow the same logic. sg-23:6 states the acceleration vector may be presented with other bracket symbols and that the components may be listed separately as long as they are labelled. sg-22:7 states that if the vector is not presented as an ordered pair, the two components must be labelled, and that reversed components lose both points.

Tables of Euler steps are labelled conditionally. sg-25:23 and sg-21:19 both state that in the presence of a correct answer a table does not need to be labelled to earn both points, and that in the presence of no answer or an incorrect answer the table must be correctly labelled.

## Parentheses [verified]

Parentheses errors are penalised once. sg-23:6 states a response with a parenthesis error in either component of a speed expression does not earn the setup point but does earn the answer point, and notes that writing the sum of two squared derivative terms without an outer square root bracket is not treated as a parenthesis error. sg-23:8 then states that parentheses errors were assessed in the earlier part and therefore will not affect the scoring in the later part.

Absolute value is treated as optional in two settings. sg-26:13 accepts an antiderivative of a logarithm written with either parentheses or absolute value, sg-23:12 states an antiderivative written without absolute value symbols is eligible for all four points, and sg-21:20 accepts either form. sg-25:25 states the ratio for the ratio test may be presented with or without absolute values, and that a response that does not use the absolute value of a ratio can earn both the ratio and the limit points.

## Simplification [verified]

The general scoring notes state that answers need not be simplified (sg-25:2, sg-26:2). The individual notes qualify this in one direction only: simplification is optional, but attempted simplification must be correct. sg-26:14 states a response does not need to simplify, but an error in simplification does not earn the answer point. sg-23:16 states the answer does not need to be simplified, however if simplification is attempted it must be correct. sg-22:13 and sg-21:17 carry the same sentence for an average rate of change.

Banking is the mechanism that protects an unsimplified correct expression. sg-25:13, sg-25:14, sg-26:3, sg-26:11, sg-25:25 and sg-26:20 all use the word for a point that later simplification cannot remove.

## Terms, ellipses and extraneous terms [verified]

Polynomials are scored on exactly the requested degree. sg-25:22 states any terms of degree greater than two or a trailing ellipsis does not earn the remaining-term point, sg-23:19 states the same for a fourth-degree polynomial with a nonzero third-degree term, and sg-23:21 for a second-degree polynomial. Series answers are treated more loosely: sg-26:22 and sg-26:23 state that extraneous terms are treated as scratch work and do not impact scoring, and sg-25:26 accepts terms presented in a list or as part of a polynomial or series.

A polynomial written in expanded form rather than in powers of the centre loses a point. sg-25:22 states that a response presenting a correct expanded form earns the first-terms point but not the remaining-term point.

## What notation never costs [verified]

The guidelines are explicit that several habits carry no penalty. Units are not required and are not read in parts that do not ask for them (sg-21:11, sg-22:15, sg-22:16, sg-26:4). The theorem behind a justification need not be named (sg-25:12, sg-26:5, sg-21:17). A derivative test need not be named (sg-26:8). Limits of integration may be present, omitted, or partially present inside integration by parts work (sg-23:18, sg-22:18). The tabular method is accepted for integration by parts with unlabelled columns (sg-23:18, sg-22:18). Extra identified critical values may be ignored (sg-26:8). Consideration of the interval endpoints does not affect scoring of a point of inflection answer (sg-25:17).
