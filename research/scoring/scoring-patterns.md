# Recurring Structures in AP Calculus BC Scoring Guidelines

Citations name the cached document and page, so `sg-25:3` is the cached 2025 scoring guidelines at page 3. For the number of questions and the sections they sit in, see [../exam/exam-structure.md](../exam/exam-structure.md).

## General scoring notes repeated on every question [verified]

Every question in sg-22, sg-23, sg-24, sg-25 and sg-26 opens with the same block. It states that the model solution uses standard mathematical notation, that answers need not be simplified, that a decimal approximation should be accurate to three places after the decimal point, and that within each individual free-response question at most one point is not earned for inappropriate rounding (sg-25:2, sg-26:2, sg-24:2, sg-23:2, sg-22:2). The wording shifted from "correct to three places" in sg-22 through sg-24 to "accurate to three places" in sg-25 and sg-26 (sg-24:2, sg-25:2).

The at-most-one-rounding-point rule is applied point by point in the later documents. sg-25:4, sg-25:7 and sg-25:23 each add that an inappropriately rounded answer does not earn the point "unless an earlier point was not earned due to inappropriate rounding", which is how the one-per-question cap is enforced inside the individual notes. The first rounding-sensitive point in a question carries the plain form with no exemption clause (sg-25:3).

Rounding is read generously in both directions. sg-25:3 accepts a value truncated rather than rounded, and accepts one third of the digits presented so long as three decimals are accurate. sg-26:4 accepts a stated integer answer plus six other truncations and the exact closed form. By contrast sg-22:3 withholds a point from a value rounded to an integer when three decimals were required, because that part carried no rounding instruction.

## How points split across the parts of a question [verified]

Each question carries nine points (sg-25:2, sg-26:2, sg-22:2). The allocation across parts is not uniform and is stated explicitly in the older format as "Total for part (a) 3 points" (sg-24:2, sg-23:2). Parts in the corpus read carry between one and five points: one point in sg-22:2 Q1(a) and sg-23:9 Q3(a), five points in sg-26:12 Q3(D) and sg-21:20 Q5(c).

The recurring split inside a part is a setup point and a value point. An integral or formula point precedes an answer point in sg-25:2 Q1(a), sg-23:3 Q1(c), sg-22:3 Q1(b) and sg-26:9 Q2(D). Where a part has three points, the middle point is normally an antiderivative or an initial condition, as in sg-25:14 Q3(d) and sg-24:3 Q1(c). Where a part has four or five points, the extra points come from a procedure with named stages, such as separation of variables (sg-26:12) or the ratio test (sg-25:24).

Question 1 and Question 2 sit in the calculator section, and Questions 3 through 6 in the non-calculator section (sg-25:2, sg-25:6, sg-25:11, sg-25:20). Questions 2, 5 and 6 carry BC-only headers in sg-25 and sg-26 (sg-25:6, sg-25:20, sg-25:24, sg-26:6, sg-26:18, sg-26:21).

## Answers with or without supporting work [verified]

The guidelines distinguish two answer points by explicit wording. "P4 is earned for the correct answer, with or without supporting work" appears at sg-25:7, and the same formula appears at sg-25:3, sg-25:10, sg-26:4, sg-26:9 and sg-26:21. The alternative label is "Answer with supporting work", which requires the supporting step to be present (sg-25:4, sg-25:8, sg-25:23).

The rubrics also name the reverse pattern, where an unsupported value earns the answer point but not the setup point. sg-26:18 states an unsupported value of the exact answer earns the answer point but not the antiderivative point, and sg-26:14 states the same for a derivative setup. sg-25:16 states a response that does not earn the derivative point can still earn the answer point through an implied application of the Fundamental Theorem.

Where the prompt demanded work, the rubric says so. sg-22:9 states an unsupported answer earns neither point, sg-23:10 states an unsupported approximation does not earn the approximation point, and sg-22:15 states a correct sum value with no supporting work earns no points.

## Linkage and communication [verified]

Linkage is the equality chain a response writes between its setup and its value. The 2025 and 2026 documents treat bad linkage as scratch work. sg-25:3 states that incorrect or unclear communication between the correct integral and the correct answer is treated as scratch work and is not considered in scoring, then gives three worked examples that each earn both points despite writing an integral equal to a divided value. sg-26:9 repeats the rule verbatim for a polar average value, and sg-25:18 applies it to a label and a value.

Earlier documents were stricter about the same pattern. sg-23:4 gives one of two points to a response that presents the average value formula in multiple steps with incomplete communication, and sg-22:3 gives one of two points to the same shape. sg-23:16 withholds the answer point from a response whose area sentence equates an integral to a negated value. This is a genuine change in treatment between sg-23 and sg-25, not a difference in question type.

A related communication rule survives across all years. An equation of the form "general expression equals numerical constant" is penalised. sg-22:6 withholds the point from a derivative expression written equal to a number without evaluation, and sg-23:6 withholds one of two points for the same shape in an acceleration vector.

## Banking [verified]

Banking is the rubric's word for a point that cannot be lost to later errors. sg-25:13 states that a correct unsimplified trapezoidal sum banks the answer point, so subsequent errors in simplification are not considered in scoring for it. sg-25:14 uses the same word for an unsimplified evaluation of an antiderivative, sg-26:3 for a midpoint sum, sg-26:11 for a tangent slope expression, sg-25:25 for the ratio in a ratio test, and sg-26:20 for limit notation on an improper integral.

Banking also runs the other way. sg-25:23 states that any subsequent error in simplification or rounding will not affect the scoring for the Euler step point, and sg-25:21 states a correct unsimplified substitution earns the second derivative point regardless of later simplification errors.

## Eligibility [verified]

Eligibility is the rubric's word for whether a later point is reachable at all. Three patterns recur.

A point may require an earlier point. sg-25:14 states that to be eligible for the answer point a response must have earned the antiderivative point. sg-25:13 states that to be eligible for the sum value a response must have earned the sum form. sg-26:8 requires the critical value point before the classification point. sg-26:16 requires the interval point before the reason point. sg-26:22 requires the first-terms point before the remaining-terms point.

A point may be blocked by a specific error even when the preceding point was earned. sg-25:4 states a response including a limit on the function rather than its derivative is not eligible to earn the value point. sg-25:25 states a reciprocal ratio earns the ratio point and keeps three later points available but is not eligible for the final interval point. sg-26:22 states that if the third-degree term is incorrect, the response is not eligible for the justification point even though it earned the evaluation point.

A point may survive the loss of its neighbour. sg-25:12 states a response does not need to earn the continuity point to be eligible for the conclusion point. sg-26:3 states the interpretation point can be earned whether or not the sum points were earned. sg-22:5 states a response that does not earn the first point is still eligible for the remaining three points of the part.

## Presentation of calculator work [verified]

In the calculator section the rubrics ask for the setup that the calculator evaluated, not the keystrokes. sg-25:2 and sg-26:9 pair "Show the setup for your calculations" with a formula point and an answer point, and sg-25:3 accepts the setup in one step or several. sg-24:5 states that to earn either speed point the setup used to perform the calculation must be evident in the response.

Degree mode is treated as a single penalty. sg-23:4 states a response using a calculator in degree mode does not earn the first point it would otherwise have earned, is generally eligible for all subsequent points, and then lists the degree-mode values so readers can identify them. The same paragraph appears at sg-23:6, sg-23:7, sg-23:8 and sg-22:3. Questions carrying trigonometric models print a radian-mode note in the stem (sg-25:2, sg-25:6, sg-26:2).

Calculator-produced values are held to the three-decimal rule, while candidate evaluations inside a justification are held to a looser one. sg-25:5 and sg-25:9 require candidate evaluations correct to the first digit after the decimal, rounded or truncated.

## Special cases and alternate solutions [verified]

Every question's notes carry a list of alternate solutions and special cases that map non-model approaches onto the same points. sg-25:5 lists two alternate justifications for a global maximum, sg-25:9 lists three, sg-26:8 gives a second derivative alternative, sg-25:21 maps a separation-of-variables route onto the product and chain rule points, and sg-23:21 maps a series-composition route onto a polynomial's three points. The label "Special case" marks a named response shape with a fixed score, as at sg-25:7, sg-25:13, sg-26:13, sg-26:19 and sg-23:14.
