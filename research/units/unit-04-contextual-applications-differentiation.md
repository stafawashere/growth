## 4.1 Interpreting the Meaning of the Derivative in Context

### Official mapping

Topic id BC-TOP-0401, CED code 4.1, name Interpreting the Meaning of the Derivative in Context, scope shared, CED page 87. [verified] (ced:87)

- BC-LO-CHA-3A (CHA-3.A): Interpret the meaning of a derivative in context.

### Concept breakdown

- BC-CON-04001 The derivative as an instantaneous rate of change in context: In a situation, the derivative says how fast the modelled quantity is changing at one instant.
- BC-CON-04002 Units of a derivative: The units of a derivative are the units of the function divided by the units of the independent variable.

- BC-SKL-04001 State the units of a derivative in context: Give the units of a rate as function units divided by independent variable units.
- BC-SKL-04002 Write a sentence interpreting a derivative value in context: Say what the number means, naming the quantity, the instant, the direction, and the units.
- BC-SKL-04003 Distinguish the value of a function from the value of its derivative in context: Say whether the question is asking how much there is or how fast it is changing.
- BC-SKL-04004 Approximate a derivative from an average rate of change with units: Use the difference quotient over the surrounding interval and report it with units.
- BC-SKL-04005 Interpret the sign and size of a derivative in context: Read a negative rate as a decrease and a large rate as a fast change.

### Required mathematical knowledge

**Derivative as an instantaneous rate.** The derivative of a function at an input is the instantaneous rate of change of the modelled quantity with respect to its independent variable at that input (BC-EK-CHA-3A1).

**Units.** The unit for the derivative is the unit for the function divided by the unit for the independent variable (BC-EK-CHA-3A3), so a second derivative carries the unit of the function divided by the square of the unit of the variable, as in words per minute per minute (sg-25:11).

**Interpretation.** A complete interpretation names the quantity, the instant or interval, the direction of change, and the units. The units are scored separately from the value in the 2024 and 2025 table based questions (sg-24:2, sg-25:11).

**Approximation of a derivative.** An average rate of change over an interval containing the input approximates the derivative there, and the approximation is presented as a difference of values divided by a difference of inputs (sg-25:11).

**Notation.** f prime of a, the derivative with respect to the named variable, and compound units written as a quotient.

### Representations

Representations in play: BC-REP-03 Numerical table, BC-REP-04 Verbal description, BC-REP-05 Contextual model.

Conversions tested: a tabulated quantity to a numerical rate with units (BC-REP-03 to BC-REP-04), and a contextual model to a sentence about an instantaneous rate (BC-REP-05 to BC-REP-04).

### Assessment behaviour

FRQ forms open a table based contextual question with an approximation of a derivative and its units, and attach an interpretation to a later part; the 2024 and 2025 questions both do this, scoring the estimate with supporting work and the units as two points (sg-24:2, sg-25:11). The 2024 question sits in the calculator active part and the 2025 one in the no-calculator part. Conceptual variants ask only for the units; computational variants ask for the estimate; interpretation variants ask for the sentence; justification variants ask why the quantity is increasing; multi-concept variants share the part with a Riemann sum or an existence theorem.

## 4.2 Straight-Line Motion: Connecting Position, Velocity, and Acceleration

### Official mapping

Topic id BC-TOP-0402, CED code 4.2, name Straight-Line Motion: Connecting Position, Velocity, and Acceleration, scope shared, CED page 88. [verified] (ced:88)

- BC-LO-CHA-3B (CHA-3.B): Calculate rates of change in applied contexts.
  - BC-EK-CHA-3B1 (CHA-3.B.1): "The derivative can be used to solve rectilinear motion problems involving position, speed, velocity, and acceleration."

Suggested practice skills: BC-MPS-1E (Practice skill 1.E). Weighting pointer: [../exam/exam-blueprint.md](../exam/exam-blueprint.md).

### Concept breakdown

- BC-CON-04003 Position, velocity, and acceleration on a line: Differentiating position gives velocity and differentiating velocity gives acceleration.
- BC-CON-04004 Velocity, speed, and direction: Velocity carries a sign that says which way the particle moves, and speed is its size with the sign removed.
- BC-CON-04005 Speeding up and slowing down: A particle speeds up when velocity and acceleration share a sign and slows down when their signs differ.

- BC-SKL-04006 Differentiate a position function to obtain velocity: Differentiate the position function once.
- BC-SKL-04007 Differentiate a velocity function to obtain acceleration: Differentiate the velocity function once.
- BC-SKL-04008 Compute the speed of a particle from its velocity: Take the size of the velocity and drop the sign.
- BC-SKL-04009 Determine the direction of motion from the sign of velocity: Positive velocity means one direction along the line and negative velocity the other.
- BC-SKL-04010 Determine whether speed is increasing from the signs of velocity and acceleration: Same signs mean speeding up and opposite signs mean slowing down.
- BC-SKL-04011 Find when a particle is at rest or changes direction: Solve for the times when the velocity is zero, then check whether the sign changes there.
- BC-SKL-04012 Communicate a sign analysis of velocity across a whole interval: Report the sign on every part of the interval, not just at sampled times.

Granularity note: Topic 4.2 carries seven skills because the single essential knowledge statement names four quantities, position, speed, velocity, and acceleration, and the scoring record separates abilities that the statement compresses. The 2025 rubric attaches separate points to considering the sign of a velocity, to analysing that sign for one particle, to analysing it for both particles across the whole interval, and to the speed conclusion, and the Chief Reader report records mean scores of 0.04 and 0.03 on the two analysis points (crabbc-25:20, crabbc-25:22). Producing velocity, producing acceleration, converting velocity to speed, reading direction, deciding whether speed increases, locating rest, and communicating the analysis therefore stand as separate testable abilities.

### Required mathematical knowledge

**Position, velocity, acceleration.** For a particle in rectilinear motion, velocity is the derivative of position and acceleration is the derivative of velocity, so acceleration is the second derivative of position (BC-EK-CHA-3B1).

**Speed.** Speed is the absolute value of velocity. Velocity carries a sign that records the direction of motion along the line; speed does not.

**Speeding up and slowing down.** Hypotheses: the velocity and the acceleration are both known at an instant. Conclusion: the speed is increasing when the two share a sign and decreasing when their signs differ. The sign of the acceleration alone does not settle the question, which the 2022 Chief Reader report names as the most common misconception on that question (cr-22:21).

**Sign analysis.** The zeros of the velocity divide the interval, the sign is constant between consecutive zeros, and a conclusion about the interval must rest on those zeros rather than on sampled inputs (crabbc-25:22).

**Notation.** x of t or s of t for position, v of t for velocity, a of t for acceleration, and the absolute value bars for speed.

Representations in play: BC-REP-01 Symbolic (analytical) expression, BC-REP-02 Graphical, BC-REP-04 Verbal description, BC-REP-05 Contextual model.

Conversions tested: a position or velocity formula to a statement about direction and speed (BC-REP-01 to BC-REP-04), and a velocity graph to a sign analysis over an interval (BC-REP-02 to BC-REP-04).

### Assessment behaviour

Calculator variants let the zeros of the velocity be found numerically; no-calculator variants keep them solvable by hand. Conceptual variants ask what speed measures; computational variants ask for a value; justification variants require both signs to be named and applied to the particle in the problem (crabbc-25:23); multi-concept variants continue into an accumulation from Unit 6.

### Required mathematical knowledge

**Common structure.** A contextual rate problem supplies a quantity as a function of an independent variable; the mathematics matches the motion case while the vocabulary must be that of the context. The CED directs that students should not use words such as velocity for the rate of change of an income, for example, even though the underlying structure is the same (ced:84).

**Units.** Each reported rate carries the units of the quantity divided by the units of the independent variable.

**End behaviour of a rate.** The long run behaviour of a rate is expressed as a limit as the independent variable increases without bound, and the limit notation must be retained rather than infinity being substituted into the expression (sg-25:4).

**Notation.** The model named for the quantity, its derivative, and the limit notation for end behaviour.

### Assessment behaviour

FRQ forms build a calculator active multipart question on one context: the 2025 BC question models an affected area with an inverse tangent function, supplies the rate expression in the stem, and asks for an average value, a time at which the instantaneous rate equals the average rate of change, a limit expression for the end behaviour of the rate, and a maximum (sg-25:2, sg-25:4). Conceptual variants ask which quantity is changing; computational variants ask for a value; interpretation variants ask for the meaning with units; multi-concept variants attach an accumulation or an extremum from Units 5 and 6 to the same context.

## 4.4 Introduction to Related Rates

### Official mapping

Topic id BC-TOP-0404, CED code 4.4, name Introduction to Related Rates, scope shared, CED page 90. [verified] (ced:90)

- BC-LO-CHA-3D (CHA-3.D): Calculate related rates in applied contexts.
  - BC-EK-CHA-3D1 (CHA-3.D.1): "The chain rule is the basis for differentiating variables in a related rates problem with respect to the same independent variable."
  - BC-EK-CHA-3D2 (CHA-3.D.2): "Other differentiation rules, such as the product rule and the quotient rule, may also be necessary to differentiate all variables with respect to the same independent variable."

Suggested practice skills: BC-MPS-1E (Practice skill 1.E). Weighting pointer: [../exam/exam-blueprint.md](../exam/exam-blueprint.md).

### Concept breakdown

- BC-CON-04007 Variables in a related rates problem as functions of time: Each changing quantity is a function of time, so each one contributes its own rate when the relation is differentiated.
- BC-CON-04008 The relating equation: Before anything can be differentiated, an equation has to tie the quantities together.

- BC-SKL-04017 Identify the variables and which rates are given and wanted: List the changing quantities, the rates you are told, and the rate you are asked for.
- BC-SKL-04018 Write an equation relating the varying quantities: Find the geometric or contextual formula that ties the quantities together.
- BC-SKL-04019 Differentiate a relation with respect to time using the chain rule: Differentiate both sides with respect to t, attaching a rate factor to every varying quantity.
- BC-SKL-04020 Apply the product rule inside a related rates differentiation: A term that multiplies two varying quantities needs the product rule.
- BC-SKL-04021 Reduce the number of variables before differentiating: Use a similar triangles proportion or a fixed dimension to write one variable in terms of another.
- BC-SKL-04022 Differentiate an implicitly defined curve with respect to time: Treat both coordinates as functions of time and differentiate the curve equation with respect to t.

### Required mathematical knowledge

**Variables as functions of time.** Each changing quantity is a function of one common independent variable, ordinarily time, and the chain rule is the basis for differentiating each of them with respect to that variable (BC-EK-CHA-3D1).

**Other rules.** The product rule and the quotient rule may also be needed to differentiate all the variables with respect to the same independent variable (BC-EK-CHA-3D2).

**The relating equation.** A geometric formula, the Pythagorean theorem, a similar triangles proportion, or the description itself supplies an equation among the quantities. Where the supplied rates cannot support two varying dimensions, one is eliminated before differentiating.

**Geometric formulas required.** Volume of a cone, volume of a sphere, volume of a cylinder, area of a circle, area of a triangle, the Pythagorean theorem, and the proportionality of corresponding sides in similar triangles.

**Notation.** dV/dt, dr/dt, dh/dt, and the marking of which rate is supplied and which is requested (ced:86).

### Representations

Representations in play: BC-REP-01 Symbolic (analytical) expression, BC-REP-05 Contextual model, BC-REP-08 Geometric diagram.

Conversions tested: a verbal scenario to a labelled diagram (BC-REP-05 to BC-REP-08), a diagram to a relating equation (BC-REP-08 to BC-REP-01), and a relating equation to an equation among rates (BC-REP-01 to BC-REP-01 with respect to time).

### Assessment behaviour

FRQ forms embed the setup in a larger question: the 2022 AB question asks for the rate of change of a cross sectional area as a vertical line travels along the base of a solid, which needs the area as a function of the horizontal position and the chain rule to reach the rate with respect to time (cr-22:6, cr-22:7), and the 2023 to 2025 implicit differentiation questions close with a related rate on a curve (cr-23:22, cr-24:17, crabbc-25:24). Conceptual variants ask which quantities vary; computational variants ask for the rate; justification variants ask for the meaning of the answer; multi-concept variants require a similar triangles reduction before the differentiation.

### Required mathematical knowledge

**Local linearity.** The tangent line at a point is the graph of a locally linear approximation of the function near the point of tangency (BC-EK-CHA-3F1), so its value at a nearby input approximates the function value there.

**Tangent line.** Hypotheses: the function is differentiable at the point. Conclusion: the tangent line through the point with slope equal to the derivative there is written in point-slope form, and isolating the dependent variable is not required (crabbc-25:25).

**Direction of the error.** Hypotheses: the concavity of the graph is known on an interval containing both the point of tangency and the nearby input. Conclusion: the tangent line lies below the curve where the graph is concave up, so the approximation underestimates, and above it where the graph is concave down, so the approximation overestimates (BC-EK-CHA-3F2). A claim about the direction that appeals to increase or decrease rather than to concavity is recorded as an observed error (BC-ERR-99020).

**Implicit curves.** When the curve is defined implicitly, the slope at the point of tangency is obtained by substituting both coordinates into the expression for the derivative (cr-24:17, crabbc-25:24).

**Notation.** Point-slope form, the approximation symbol, and the second derivative sign as the stated reason.

## 4.7 Using L'Hospital's Rule for Determining Limits of Indeterminate Forms

### Official mapping

Topic id BC-TOP-0407, CED code 4.7, name Using L'Hospital's Rule for Determining Limits of Indeterminate Forms, scope shared, CED page 93. [verified] (ced:93)

- BC-LO-LIM-4A (LIM-4.A): Determine limits of functions that result in indeterminate forms.
  - BC-EK-LIM-4A1 (LIM-4.A.1): "When the ratio of two functions tends to 0/0 or infinity/infinity in the limit, such forms are said to be indeterminate."
  - BC-EK-LIM-4A2 (LIM-4.A.2): "Limits of the indeterminate forms 0/0 or infinity/infinity may be evaluated using L'Hospital's Rule."

Suggested practice skills: BC-MPS-3D (Practice skill 3.D). Weighting pointer: [../exam/exam-blueprint.md](../exam/exam-blueprint.md).

### Concept breakdown

- BC-CON-04013 Indeterminate form: Zero over zero and infinity over infinity are labels for a limit that is not yet settled, not values.
- BC-CON-04014 The hypothesis of L'Hospital's rule: The rule may be used only after the form has been shown to be indeterminate.
- BC-CON-04015 The conclusion of L'Hospital's rule: The rule replaces the limit of a ratio by the limit of the ratio of the two derivatives.

- BC-SKL-04033 Verify that a limit has an indeterminate form: Show the limit of the top and the limit of the bottom separately, and say the form is indeterminate.
- BC-SKL-04034 State L'Hospital's rule with its hypotheses: Say when the rule may be used and what it concludes.
- BC-SKL-04035 Apply the rule by differentiating numerator and denominator separately: Differentiate the top and the bottom on their own, not as a quotient.
- BC-SKL-04036 Evaluate the limit that the rule produces: Work out the new limit and report the value.
- BC-SKL-04037 Apply the rule again when the form is still indeterminate: If the new quotient is still indeterminate, check the form again and repeat.
- BC-SKL-04038 Use differentiability to evaluate a limit of a given function: A function differentiable at a point is continuous there, so its limit is its value.

### Required mathematical knowledge

**Indeterminate form.** When the ratio of two functions tends to zero over zero or infinity over infinity in the limit, the form is said to be indeterminate (BC-EK-LIM-4A1). The CED exclusion statement on the same page removes other indeterminate forms, such as infinity minus infinity, from assessment on either exam (ced:93).

**The hypothesis.** The CED unit overview states that students must verify that the limits of the numerator and of the denominator are both zero, or that both approach infinity, as a necessary first step before applying the rule, and that it is incorrect to write the limit of the quotient equal to zero over zero (ced:84). The 2023 rubric awards a point for presenting the two limits separately and withholds it from a response that writes the quotient explicitly equal to the indeterminate form (sg-23:14).

**The conclusion.** Under the hypothesis, the limit of the quotient equals the limit of the quotient of the derivative of the numerator and the derivative of the denominator. The CED emphasises that this is the ratio of the derivatives and not the derivative of the ratio (ced:84).

**Continuity from differentiability.** Where the numerator contains a function known only through supplied properties, differentiability at the point gives continuity there, so the limit of that function is its value; the 2023 model solution opens with exactly this step (sg-23:14).

**Notation.** Limit notation retained throughout, with the two limits written as separate statements.
